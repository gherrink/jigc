## How to read them

- **`decisions.md` is what counts.** The sheet's *Recommendation* lines are its author's, and the options a facts file closes with are its explorer's. The human ruled otherwise than the sheet recommended at five questions — items 6, 7 with 8, 15, 19 and 21 — and at the cross-model pass he took more than the orchestrator recommended. `DECISIONS.md` says so at each.
- **Item numbers are the ledger's**: 1–20 are group (a), 21–23 are group (b) 1–3. The sheet and the record use the same numbers.
- **The sheet is a reading, not a measurement.** Its own head says it: no build, no test, no binary; every situation is read from the code, a named test, a commit message or a fixer's report. A fixer that builds a ruling reproduces the situation first.
- **The spike is a measurement**, of one candidate (`b913e602`) on one machine. Its logs, scripts and JUnit files stayed under its scratch root and are not copied; the method is in the file, command by command.
