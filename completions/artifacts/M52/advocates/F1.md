# Fork 1 — the robust case (independent advocate). Verdict: **robust-now**, with one concession.

Everything below marked **[drove]** I ran myself on `~/.local/bin/jigc` = 1.0.0-rc.15, rig
`committed-singletons`, repo HEAD `85ad06c5`. **[read]** is a source read at the cited line.

## 1 · The position

The cheap arm is not "the same fix, cheaper." It is **a different subject**. Patch-per-site takes the
subject to be *the six sites the review and baseline happened to name*; the robust arm takes it to be
*every place jigc puts bytes back*. The wave whose claim is M45's complete-fix contract cannot ship a
fix whose axis is a list of reported repros — that is the exact shape this repo has re-found in five
consecutive audits. A registry is not gilding here: it **is** the axis, and there is no other
candidate, because `grep ROLLBACK_|RESTORE_` → 0 and no clap tree bijects this class.

## 2 · The hole the cheap arm leaves, and what byte dies — driven

**`unwind_mint` (population 8) — I reproduced it. [drove]**

```
hook: mkdir -p $PWD/.jigc/tasks/area-high/docs
      printf 'THIRD PARTY PROSE — hours of authored work\n' > .../docs/authored.md; echo HOOKNO >&2; exit 1
$ jigc milestone add-task advocate-probe-milestone 'area high'          → exit 1
  nothing was committed — the record append and the sub-task mint were both rolled back,
  so milestone:advocate-probe-milestone is unchanged.
$ [ -f .jigc/tasks/area-high/docs/authored.md ]                         → NO — DESTROYED
$ ls .jigc/tasks/                                                       → empty
```

The dead bytes are **authored prose in the gitignored `.jigc/tasks/<id>/docs/`** — no object DB, no
`.jigc/displaced/` park, no `note:`, no finding. And the frame is a **law-1 lie in the wave's own
terms**: *"nothing was committed … the milestone is unchanged"* is printed over a destruction. The
cheap arm leaves this exactly as driven, on `finalize.md:195`'s carve-out — whose rationale is
*"including files this run never saw."* `unwind_mint`'s directory is one **this call created three
lines up**; every file in it this run never saw is, by construction, somebody else's. The carve-out's
warrant is false here, verbatim.

The sharper cell is `add-from-spec`/`unwind_unrecorded_seeds` **[read** `milestone.rs:1550-1580]**:
it mints **N sub-tasks up front**, prints their ids, then lands N record-only commits. A rejected
k-th commit `remove_dir_all`s every un-recorded area — a window spanning N−1 commits and N−1 hook
runs, in the one door that exists to seed a **fan-out**, where the third party is not a hook but
*another jigc agent* writing into `tasks/<sub>/docs/` (`storage.md` → Concurrent writers' own
carve-out). The cheap arm leaves that population untouched too.

**`config set <root-knob>` (the tenth population)** has **no rollback of any kind** and is not in the
cheap arm's six. Baseline §2.6: the `git mv`s stage, the file-state baseline re-keys, the knob write
fails, and a managed doc leaves `doc list` and `doc show` at exit 1 while `validate` grades it
advisory at exit 0.

**`retire` is worse than "no declared behaviour to keep."** `task.rs:4915-4922` restores **only
`if !abs.exists()` [read]**. Where a racer recreated the retired path, the original's bytes are gone
and jigc says nothing — the CAS *conflict* cell, silently taken as a no-op. Striking `finalize.md:174`
"to match the binary" **ratifies a silent-discard cell in a doc whose next paragraph is the
no-silent-discard promise.** The honest doc act is to state the CAS rule there, which only the robust
arm makes true.

## 3 · The one-way-door tell — two doors, both closing at the 1.0 pin

1. **The finding identity.** `command-output-contract.md:197-204` pins `{code, target}` as *the stable
   per-instance identity*; the evolution posture (`:480`) says **"A removal or a reshape is `2.0`."**
   Shipping `finalize.rollback-conflict` from `rename`, `milestone create`, `task discard`, `doc
   author` and `config set` — none of them finalize doors — pins a code that **lies about its own
   door** for the life of `1.x`. A driver deduping on `(code, target)` cannot tell a raced rename from
   a raced finalize. Renaming it after the pin is a major.
2. **The `{error}` arm.** DEFECT 1's machine half is not a stream nicety: the single-key `{error}`
   arm **cannot carry a key at all**, so every rollback conflict is invisible to the machine surface.
   M51 Increment 6 established that a **reshape of a declared arm is admissible only while the window
   is open** (`:490-494`), and M52 is the last wave before the call. The cheap arm's answer —
   *suppress it under `--format json`* — spends the last window to make the silence **permanent and
   declared**, on a finding whose entire content is *"bytes you cannot otherwise find are parked at a
   gitignored path."*

## 4 · On the committed trajectory, not hypothetical

`finalize.md:200`: *"before phase 6, nothing jigc wrote survives a failure except where surviving it
is the only way to avoid destroying someone else's bytes, **and every such survival is named**."*
That is a VISION-level invariant (*detected and routed, never silently merged*; *no silent discard*)
written as a promise about **jigc's transactions**, and the doc scopes it to *the finalize
transaction* only because in M51 nobody had counted the other five. G-3 is right that this is a doc
gap and not a foreclosure — which means extending the promise to *every jigc transaction* **costs a
sentence and forecloses nothing.** Refusing to extend it is the choice that needs a warrant.

## 5 · The two rationalizations, refuted — and one I refuted by driving

**"No live case."** Dead: I drove the worst cell in §2 in four commands, and the baseline drove six
more. Nothing here is hypothetical.

**"Premature generality — a registry is over-engineering."** I drove the counter-example **against the
cheap arm's own DEFECT-1 fix**. Trimming `.jigc/.gitignore`, racing it from a hook, `--format json`:

```
$ jigc task finalize advocate-probe-json-conflict --format json     → exit 1, stdout 0 B
stderr:  finalize — about to commit the index; leaving out:          ← LEADING prose
           .jigc/milestones/.../tasks.json  (+3)
         { "error": "`git commit` was rejected …" }                  ← the envelope
         blocking · finalize.rollback-conflict — `.jigc/.gitignore` …← TRAILING prose
$ python3 -c "json.load(open(err))"  → JSONDecodeError: line 1 column 1        [drove]
```

The cheap arm suppresses the **trailing** block. The stream is still unparseable at the identical
command, because of a **leading** producer the baseline's census never counted — and here is why it
could not: `emit_left_out_advisory` (`task.rs:4709`) **does consult `Format`**, and routes to stderr
*because of* `--format json`, on the doc-comment premise *"the structured envelope owns stdout"*
(`task.rs:4705-4708` **[read]**). That premise is **true on the success path and false on the reject
path**, where stdout is empty and the envelope is on stderr. Three more producers obey the same wrong
rule: `emit_carried_advisory` (`task.rs:4725`), `relay_hook_output` (`task.rs:3438`), and
`carry_rollback_conflicts` itself. So DEFECT 1's class is **≥4 producers at one door**, not 2 across
two — and the baseline's grep (*prints without consulting `Format`*) is **blind to all but one of
them by construction**. A per-site patch here is not cheaper; it is **measurably wrong**, and the rule
that catches it — *the JSON-bearing stream is stdout, except at reject* — is exactly the kind of thing
a registry row forces someone to write down.

## 6 · "Remove only what this call minted" needs **no capture** — measured

`mint_task` writes exactly three files (`state.rs:633-649`: `BASE_PIN_FILE`, `INTENT_FILE`,
`WORKFLOW_FILE`) and `mint_milestone` exactly two (`base.json`, `tasks.json`). Driven, a fresh mint's
whole output is:

```
.jigc/tasks/area-high/{base.json, intent, workflow}                                     [drove]
```

The minted set is a **compile-time constant of the mint**, so the unwind is: remove those names, then
`std::fs::remove_dir` (**non-recursive**). `ENOTEMPTY` ⇒ a third party is in there ⇒ leave the bytes,
name the leftover, route. That idiom is already in this codebase twelve hundred lines away —
`task.rs:4897`: *"`remove_dir` only succeeds on an EMPTY dir, so a dir holding other ADRs survives."*
Roughly five lines per site, no capture, no new type. The honest cost is a **re-opened
`serial-collision` on the raced re-run** — which is a *named, routed block* replacing *silent
destruction of unrecoverable prose*, and is the trade M47 Inc 2 would have made had anyone asked.

## 7 · What `storage.md` actually forbids

`storage.md` → Concurrent writers forbids exactly two things: a torn read of the three shared cache
files (closed by `state::persist`), and **a subprocess inside the save-lock critical section** (the
nested `jigc validate` pre-commit hook deadlocking its parent). It says nothing about capture-and-
compare-before-restore, which takes no lock and spawns nothing. M51's §2 read it as *"single-writer at
a work unit"* to refuse a descriptor-held traversal — and **M51's own D4 falsifies that reading in the
same settle**: it builds a CAS precisely because *"the interval spans promotion, retirement, ignore
maintenance, staging and hooks"* — arbitrary third-party code, inside the transaction, by design.
`finalize.md` names the hook as **the** instrument. G-5's blocking question therefore has a
non-arbitrary answer: the §2 reading is about **jigc processes**; it was never a claim that the
worktree has one writer, and §2's own stated reopening condition (*"if a door ever writes the worktree
concurrently with finalize"*) is satisfied at HEAD by the hook and by the fan-out.

## 8 · The priced cost of the cheap arm

- **Two populations keep destroying unrecoverable bytes at exit 1 with a lying frame** (`unwind_mint`,
  `unwind_unrecorded_seeds`) — and the second is the fan-out seeding door.
- **One population keeps having no rollback at all** (`config set <root-knob>`).
- **A wrong finding identity freezes at 1.0**, so the repair is a `2.0` act; the acknowledge-ledger
  the contract forward-declares inherits a key that cannot discriminate five doors.
- **The `{error}` silence becomes declared**, so the *only* signal that a pre-image is parked in a
  gitignored directory never reaches a machine driver — for the life of `1.x`.
- **The stream stays broken at the door the fix was written for** (§5) — a regression-in-disguise: the
  wave ships a DEFECT-1 fix and DEFECT 1 still reproduces, one command, same argv.
- **`finalize.md:174` struck rather than repaired** leaves a locked doc with a silent-discard cell
  ratified in prose, which is the tier-0 shape M52 exists to remove.

## 9 · The robust scope — minimal, not maximal

1. **A `ROLLBACK_POPULATIONS` registry**, one row per population, each carrying its discipline:
   `FileCas` · `MintedSet(<the mint's own constant file list>)` · `DoorGuard(<named guard>)` ·
   `Declared(<reason>)`. Fence = a counted source scan on the `repo_relative_paths.rs:849` mold
   (10 production restore fns + the two non-fn restores `Drop for RecordFlipGuard` and
   `rollback_promotions`' inline `fs::write`). **~9 rows + 1 fence.**
2. **Generalize the shipped CAS**, do not re-write it. `ConfigLayerPreImage.spec: &'static str` →
   a runtime identity used for the family key and the park filename; `ConfigLayerHome` → an absolute
   path. The ~150-line `restore` body (`task.rs:4190-4243`) is **unchanged** — its logic is already
   correct and already handles absent-pre-image, unreadable and `pre == post`. Apply to populations
   2/3/4/5/6.
3. **The conflict code takes its door** — a `&'static str` parameter at the finding constructor, so
   `rename.rollback-conflict` / `record.rollback-conflict` discriminate. One parameter, six callers.
4. **`MintedSet` unwind** at the two `unwind_mint` sites — §6, ~5 lines each, no capture.
5. **The reject envelope carries findings** (fork 6's declared `Reject::Findings` reshape, which M52
   is taking anyway) + **the stream rule stated once and applied at all four `Format`-routing
   producers**, since a per-site patch demonstrably misses three of them.
6. **`finalize.md:200`'s promise reads *every jigc transaction*; `:174` states the CAS rule; `:193`'s
   door count becomes the registry, not a list** (G-4's three-way disagreement dies with it).

The only genuinely new construction is (1) and the five-line (4). (2) is a type change over working
code; (3) is a parameter; (5) and (6) are already owed by fork 6 and G-3/G-4.

## 10 · Where the cheap arm is right — conceded

**Two populations are already correct and need only a declared row, not a mechanism.** `jigc setup`'s
pre-write refusal (M51 Inc 3) — I re-confirmed the class's sibling cell untouched **[drove**, baseline
§2.5 cell 4**]** — and `CreatedDoc::rollback`, whose window is intra-process and therefore has no
third-party racer at all. A `DoorGuard` / `Declared` row is sufficient there, and the robust arm
should *not* grow a mechanism for them.

**But `rename` is not the third such population, and the fork statement's framing slightly overstates
it.** `rename.dirty-tree` makes only the `tracked_restore`-to-HEAD arm admissible. The *other two arms
of the same function* — the unconditional `remove_file` of the new path (`rename.rs:825-828`) and the
unconditional rewrite of `.jigc/state/file-state.json` (`:832-838`) — are not covered by that guard at
all, and the baseline drove the racer's bytes dying on the first. So `rollback_rename` needs the CAS
for two of three arms and a `DoorGuard` row for the third — which is, precisely, the thing a registry
row can say and a per-site patch cannot.

And the registry row on a `DoorGuard` population is not decoration even where the code is right today:
population 6's HEAD-sourced restore is *only* safe while `rename.dirty-tree` refuses first, and
nothing at HEAD connects those two facts. A row naming the guard is the connection.

## Verdict

**robust-now.** Not because the robust arm is more thorough — because the cheap arm's *subject is
wrong*, and I drove that rather than argued it: its own DEFECT-1 fix leaves DEFECT 1 reproducing at
the identical command, because the class is `Format`-routing producers acting on a premise that is
false at reject, and the baseline's census cannot see them. Two populations keep destroying bytes that
exist in no object DB behind a frame that says nothing was destroyed. And two of the cheap arm's
choices — the one finding identity and the `{error}` silence — are **one-way doors that close at the
1.0 pin this wave precedes**, making the same repair a `2.0` act. The robust arm's genuinely new
construction is one registry, one type generalization over working code, one parameter, and ten lines
of `remove_dir`.
