## The unseeded-honesty statement

**The clean-room held at the content level; three bounded seams, all declared or found, none feeding workers.**

1. **Declared route seeding (protocol):** P1's prompt named `jigc setup` and `jigc config set invocation-log true` — the two commands that physically cannot be tool-guided (the log knob doesn't exist before setup). Everything after record 1 was tool-guided or worker-initiated.
2. **One non-worker record in the log (found by this analysis):** record 343 — `validate --format json`, 05:32:04Z, the *same second* as the plant commit `d738126` and 2 minutes after P3's last worker record — is the operator's post-plant verification, not a worker invocation. It used `--format json` (P3's worker used plain `validate` for its wrap-up), fired `schema-version-ahead` before any worker session had started post-plant, and the P3 feedback mentions no exit-1. So the log is **503/504 pure worker records**, not 504/504 as the protocol intended. It leaked nothing to any worker (P4's session started fresh 35 s later) but is a protocol breach worth the line.
3. **Operator raw-git by design, unlogged:** P2's staging plant and post-capture restore, and the P4 stamp plant — all outside jigc, per protocol.
4. **Content-level leak check — negative.** No feedback report references any prior dashboard trial, any prior finding vocabulary, or the existence of designed traps. P2's worker treated the planted staged files as ordinary workspace state and reasoned from the composed prose alone. P4's worker deliberated workflow choice from scratch (burned a round on two previews) and never named the trap. P1 independently rediscovered findings that prior (unseen) trials also hit — the owner-artifact root ambiguity, the `--preview` refusal — which is replication, and it also *contradicted* prior-session knowledge where the tool had moved (complaining of a missing unset that exists since M41), which an archive-seeded session would not do. The strongest positive evidence of blindness is P3: the worker chose defensive append-only ordering *because it could not know* author's collision behavior — knowledge every seeded prior session had.
5. **P3's prompt** carried an operator-authored instruction beyond the protocol's illustrative example (the worker refers to "a document you had explicitly told me to leave untouched") — operator-authored, not archive-derived; noted for completeness.
