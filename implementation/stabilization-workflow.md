# Stabilization workflow (open → test → rule → fix → close)

The loop for **taking a build to a version ready to release**. It takes a build and a **closing condition**, finds and fixes until the condition holds, records everything else, and ends with one release ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The stabilization workflow, as ruled*, ruling 1 — the entry every ruling number below refers to). It is the workflow form of a loop that was run by hand on one release candidate — test, fix, re-test — and did not converge: [the record of that pass](../completions/artifacts/M55/fix-pass-rc25/README.md), and the parked description this doc replaces, [ideas/gate-loop-workflow.md](../ideas/gate-loop-workflow.md).

**Where it sits.** The five other loops bracket the `milestone > increment > task` hierarchy: [design](design-workflow.md) governs a *decision*, [dev](dev-workflow.md) a *task*, [milestone planning](milestone-planning-workflow.md) the *opening of a milestone*, [increment](increment-workflow.md) an *increment*, [milestone completion](milestone-completion-workflow.md) a milestone's *close*. This one stands outside that hierarchy. Its unit is a **run** — one build, whatever milestones and fix passes it carries, held against one closing condition — and a run is made of **rounds**. It is the customer of two of the others and restates neither: the completion workflow's audit, verify-real and fix lane, and the dev workflow's loop and gate. It is prose (this doc), a harness script and agent definitions, **not a jigc pack workflow** — that migration is later (ruling 1).

**This doc is the one home of how a run is operated**: what the orchestrator does at each step and on each answer. A rule another doc owns is pointed at, and a mechanism the harness or the record script owns is named with its purpose and left where it is — **Who owns which rule**, below, is the map.

## What is built, and what is not

**Built and committed:** the gate's tiers and its pre-check ([`dev/gate`](../dev/gate)); the record script ([`dev/stabilize-record`](../dev/stabilize-record)) and its suite; the step tool ([`dev/stabilize-step`](../dev/stabilize-step)) — every git act of a run as one command, the digest of every act, and the long commands a stage holds — and its suite, which drives each act against real git; the agent definitions a run calls (`.claude/agents/`); the harness ([`.claude/workflows/stabilize.js`](../.claude/workflows/stabilize.js)) and the fence over its source and over every prompt it can compose; a simulation of the `test` stage, which runs the committed harness through a whole stage on every gate (→ The simulation of a stage); the first part of the regression set ([`dev/regression-set`](../dev/regression-set)) with its suite, its list and the records of its three runs (→ The regression set); the runtime probes — invocations of the harness that work on no run, each observing one fact about the runtime, and the tool that judges what they see ([`dev/stabilize-probe`](../dev/stabilize-probe)) with its suite (→ The runtime probes); and the setup of the small canary ([`dev/stabilize-canary`](../dev/stabilize-canary)) with its suite (→ The small canary).

**Built is not yet fit for use.** An independent, read-only review of the build came back red, and the human ruled the order of its repair: **no stage is used on a real run until the repair of its half and the re-review of that repair are recorded** in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) — **the `test` half first** ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The stabilization workflow's build, reviewed red*, ruling 3). Where each half stands is said once, under **Open questions and declared bounds** → *Where the two halves stand*. In one line each: **the `test` half** was repaired, re-reviewed on 2026-10-07 — *fit for a canary run, not yet for the real run* — and repaired a second time at the scale the human ruled that day: its core is built, everything else is a recorded row with a due point, and **no stage has yet run under the runtime with real agents** — the small canary is that run. **The `fix` half is not repaired, and refuses to start** ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The `fix` stage refuses to start*): an invocation of `stage: 'fix'` returns `refused` with `not_fit` before any agent runs — whatever its arguments, an exit at a bound included — and its message names the build's record. **One invocation of it is not refused, because it starts no stage:** the one that only records what the human ruled about the run — a go, one more re-run, either bound raised, one more triage, one more attempt (`go`, `rerun`, `rounds`, `cycles`, `reverify`, `again`) — which either stage takes. A ruling on a finding, or a declared bound, is the `fix` stage's own first step and waits with it — **and until that stage is fit it is recorded by an invocation of `test` that carries only `rulings`**, which records them on the loop branch through the one step that may write them and starts nothing ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The record step, rebuilt*). The refusal is one line of the harness (`NOT_FIT`), deleted by the commit that records the `fix` half's re-review. **Nothing refuses the `test` stage on that ground: that it is not used on a real run before its record says so is the orchestrator's rule to keep.** Where this doc says what a step does, it says what the code does and what the rulings require; what a *real agent* does with a prompt is observed for the runtime probes alone, and is marked as not observed everywhere else until the canary's record says otherwise.

**Not built, or not yet true** — each is taken up where it bites, and listed whole under **Open questions and declared bounds**:

- **Two of the instruments a closing condition can name are not whole: the regression set, of which the first of two parts is built (→ The regression set), and the port rehearsal, of which nothing is** (ruling 16). A run whose condition has a clause only one of them could judge cannot close until that instrument is built and has its row in the test set (→ Close).
- **The checks on the release PR's head are a procedure the orchestrator runs, not a stage of the harness**, and only one of its three checks is a command today (→ Close).
- **One stage has run under the Workflow runtime with real agents — the small canary's `test` stage, on 2026-10-08 — and no stage of a real run.** What stands behind the harness: the fence over its source and over every prompt it can compose; its own self-test, which launches no agent; the simulation, whose runtime is a stand-in and whose agents are functions (→ The simulation of a stage); the step tool's suite, which drives every act against real git on every gate; and — **observed under the runtime on 2026-10-07** — the self-test and the runtime probes: that the runtime takes the script, that a digest is relayed whole, what a return that lacks a required field costs, and that a long command is held (→ The runtime probes). **A whole stage was first shown by the small canary**, in a clone with a bare remote of its own (→ The small canary, *What it showed*): it reached its record and its push, and it did not end — verification multiplies, which is open with the human. Round 1 of the first run, which by the human's ruling is the tuning round, is the next to show one. What each showed is recorded in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) and in the records it names, never here.
- **The `stabilize/` branch type is not applied.** Runs use names the branch rule accepts today (→ Branches).
- **The step in which the human rules on findings found outside the test set is written to be replaceable by an agent** — and is the human's until the human says otherwise (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks).

## The loop (one run)

1. **Open** — a human-led step in the main session fixes what the run is: the closing condition, the candidate and the previous release, the test set, the declared bounds, the stop mode and the bounds. One commit (→ Opening a run).
2. **Test** — one invocation of the harness's `test` stage on the current candidate: the deterministic checks in full, the model-driven instruments over what changed, triage of every finding, verify-real, one record commit (→ A round's `test`).
3. **Rule** — where the state asks for the human: the list of verified findings found outside the test set, a fork, a clause that is still not green after its re-run, a stop. Always **between** two invocations, never inside one (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks).
4. **Fix** — one invocation of the `fix` stage: the human's rulings recorded, fixers, the audit of the fix diff, at most three such cycles, then the round **lands** — or the stage returns at a bound with the round unlanded (→ A round's `fix` · Bounds and exits).
5. **Again** — a landed round leaves a new candidate that nobody has tested, so the next round's `test` comes first, over what the fixes reach. The loop ends when the state answers `close`.
6. **Close** — the closing acts, one pull request, the human's merge, the checks on the release PR's head, the human's merge of the release PR, the publish, the registry proof (→ Close).

**Between any two steps the orchestrator does one thing: it reads `next`** — which a stage that finished returns, and which `dev/stabilize-record state --run <run>` prints at any time, for the branch that is checked out — and does what **Reading the next step** says for that value. It never decides the step itself, and never carries a round, a cycle or a verdict from one invocation to the next: each invocation reads the committed state (ruling 9).

## Invoking the harness

**By its script's path, never by its name.** A session loads a workflow it invokes by name **once**, and runs that copy for as long as it lives — after the file is edited too, and without a word. Observed on 2026-10-07: the self-test reported 373 checks invoked by name and 421 invoked by path, in a session that had edited the harness between the two ([probes/README.md](../completions/artifacts/M55/stabilization-build/re-review-test-half/probes/README.md) → *Three things no probe was built for*). Nothing in the harness can know that it is stale. So **every invocation this doc shows names the script**, the harness as the repository the session stands in holds it:

```text
Workflow({ scriptPath: '<the repository's root>/.claude/workflows/stabilize.js', args: { … } })
```

`args` is a stage (→ A round's `test` · A round's `fix`), the self-test (`{ selfTest: true }`, which launches no agent), or one runtime probe (→ The runtime probes) — the harness's header, *Usage*, is the home of every field. That header's own usage lines, and the usage a refused invocation is answered with, name the script's path too (since the commit that finished `X1b`).

- **`scratch` is an absolute directory of plain segments, outside the repository.** Every agent of the invocation works under it, and the step tool keeps under it what a digest leaves out, what a held command prints, the verdict it read, and the commit step's kept answer. **What is kept under a scratch root is found under that root only** — an invocation that is to finish what another left is handed the same one (a declared bound).
- **A stage runs in whatever repository its session stands in.** Nothing in an invocation names the repository: every agent's shell starts in the session's directory. A stage invoked where no such run is opened halts at its first step with nothing written — which is what the canary's safety rests on (→ The small canary).
- **No agent of a stage is to meet a permission prompt**, and nothing but a run shows that none does (→ Reading the next step, *What a stage's agent types*).
## Opening a run

A run opens with a **human-led step in the main session**, as milestone planning does ([milestone-planning-workflow.md](milestone-planning-workflow.md) → Orchestration; ruling 14). Forks go to the human one at a time; reading, checking and writing go to subagents (ruling 17).

**What it decides, and writes as the opening record** — `opening.md` in the run's directory, prose that the record script neither writes nor reads:

- the **closing condition**, by reference to the decision that states it, clause by clause, each with its scope and its instrument;
- the **candidate** and the **previous release** the run measures against, and the **release being prepared**;
- the **test set** per instrument — fixed before round 1 (ruling 4);
- the **declared bounds** — each the human's ruling, with its reach, and pinned by a test or said to be unpinned (ruling 5);
- the **stop mode** and the **bound across rounds** (ruling 6: the workflow's first run stops after every round, so that it can be tuned; a later run stops at the bound, three rounds unless its opening writes another number), and the **default scope** of a round;
- the **ledger's opening rows**: what is already known against the candidate.

**The run's slug** names its directory under `completions/artifacts/` and, through the harness's one name function, its branches — so it is chosen together with the loop branch (→ Branches).

**What goes in as data, and by which command.** Everything a later step computes from is a row the record script wrote; nothing is read out of the opening record's prose. The script's `--help` owns each call's fields.

| Fact | Call | What computes from it |
|---|---|---|
| the stop mode, the bound across rounds, the previous release's version and the commit it was released from, the default scope — and, where the run is to have another than three, the bound on a round's fix cycles | `dev/stabilize-record run-set` | where the run stops for the human; the second binary of every round and what *a regression of the run* is measured against; the scope of a round whose invocation names none |
| **the clauses of the closing condition** — the run's census, written once | `dev/stabilize-record run-set` (`clauses`) | closing — the clause table has a row per clause of the census from that call on, each *void* until an item that judges it has run; a run with no census is not ready; and a clause's row that is later missing from the table is a refusal, never a clause nothing asks about |
| the test set, one row per instrument item: its kind, the clause it judges, whether it runs on every candidate or only in scope, the doors and registries it covers, its brief | `dev/stabilize-record item-set` | which items a round runs; which agents run each; **and every clause's status** — so a row's clause is a clause of the census or the row is refused, and what the derivation reads of a row stands once a round has run the item or owes it a run (→ A round's `test`, *The derivation's inputs are held*) |
| the declared bounds | `dev/stabilize-record bound-set` | whether a grade of *out of scope* may stand |
| the opening rows of the ledger, as round 0 | `dev/stabilize-record ledger-add` | the first triage that runs is handed them |

Four things about those calls that the opening has to get right:

- **What the opening writes is written once.** The stop mode, the previous release, the default scope and the clauses stand as first written — a second, different value is refused — and each of the two bounds is written at the opening and after that only *raised*, at the stop that names it (→ Bounds and exits). So the opening is the one place to get them right: a run whose condition or stop mode changes is opened again.
- **The script creates neither the run's directory nor the opening record.** Both exist before the first call; a mistyped run name is refused and mints nothing.
- **Every write is scanned**, so the machine needs gitleaks and the private denylist ([public-hygiene.md](public-hygiene.md) → The guard), beside python3, git and bash. Without them nothing is written — **and nothing is checked, committed or pushed by a step**, which vets with the same scanners (→ The record step, *What is vetted, and where*) — and that is reported as *did not run*, never as a pass. [machine-setup.md](machine-setup.md) does not list them yet.
- **An item's kind decides who runs it.** The harness has a chain of agents for a review row, a trial arm and the three kinds of the audit (an area, the cross-cutting pass, the drive); an item of kind `check` is a deterministic check, run by the preflight from the item's brief; an item of kind `held-<id>` is a check that is one long command, which `dev/stabilize-step` holds and judges and whose result is that tool's verdict (→ Reading the next step, *A scripted check is a kind of item*) — the stage starts it itself, after the candidate's gate, and a `held-gate` is answered from that gate; any other kind halts the stage that would run it. The names are the keys of the harness's `CHAINS` table, which is the list — this sentence is not.

**Then one commit, behind the full gate, pushed by name.** The opening is ready when `dev/stabilize-record state --run <run>` prints `"not_ready": []` and `"next": "test"`; until then `next` is `not-ready`, `not_ready` names what is owed, and both stages are refused. From this commit on, the harness holds the loop branch to its path rule (→ Branches).

**Each run's opening is its own decision.** This doc opens none, and the first run's is recorded where it is taken.

## A round's `test`

```text
Workflow({ scriptPath: '<the repository's root>/.claude/workflows/stabilize.js', args: { stage: 'test', run: '<run>', scratch: '<absolute dir outside the repository>' } })
```

The loop branch is checked out and holds nothing but the run's pending writes — and **what its remote lacks of it is a record's commit, or it is somebody's to push first**: the read every stage starts from makes the push a record is owed, and refuses to start over a commit that is no record's (→ The record step, *A record act is repeatable*). The check that reads CI needs a run for exactly the candidate's commit, which is one more reason the candidate is pushed before anything runs. The candidate of a round a stage begins is the loop branch's tip at the invocation — a re-run's is its round's, never the tip (→ Reading the next step, *A round tests one candidate*) — and its label and the round's number are derived, never passed. What the machine needs beyond the gate's tools the preflight asserts, and halts naming what is missing: the record script's scanners, a container runtime where a trial arm runs, `gh` where a check reads CI.

**What the stage does, in order** (the harness's header is the home of the list; each role's contract is its definition):

1. **The git state** — and the path rule of → Branches, asserted — and the run's state: a stage the state's position refuses starts nothing.
2. **The attempt, begun on record.** One step (`dev/stabilize-step begin`) holds the round and the attempt to the state's position once more and writes the attempt's marker — a report of the harness's own — before any agent is launched. So an attempt whose agents all died before one of them wrote is still an attempt the record script counts (→ Halt and resume), **and no stage begins on a run whose opening is not done**: the tool refuses it, whatever the harness read.
3. **The preflight, then what the tool holds — beside the scope step.** *The preflight* asserts the environment — first, so that an assert that fails stops the stage before anything is built — and runs the deterministic checks that are a brief: the test set's items of kind `check`; in a round that runs a trial arm a second one builds and verifies the one trial image. **It builds no binary and holds no gate.** Three long commands follow it, each *held by `dev/stabilize-step`* (→ Reading the next step, *What a stage's agent types, and how a long command is held*): **one** release binary from the candidate's commit, the previous release's binary from its recorded commit, and **the candidate's own full gate, once** — the tool's held `gate`, which is `dev/gate --keep-going`, so that no red step stops it — whose whole output the tool keeps: what it shows red is what every record commit of the round is held to (→ The record step). Then, once the scope is read back, **the round's held checks, one after another** — the regression set after the gate, never beside it. *Scope* resolves what this round tests into **doors**, from the code and the docs as they stand: the doors the change reaches and the doors it does not, each with its derivation, written once. Where the consumers of a change cannot be enumerated to an end, the whole registry is inside (ruling 10).
4. **The instruments** the round's doors select, in parallel: the record script computes which items run — an every-candidate item always, an in-scope item when the round's doors reach it. Every agent that drives — a reviewer verifying its findings among them — is handed the one binary and returns the hash it asserted; the harness compares the two (ruling 11), and what an agent drove on a binary it did not assert is evidence about nothing — its item is void (→ *How an agent can end*, below).
5. **The reports, checked** — after the instruments, after every triage pass, and once more inside the record's batch. Every report is meant to reach the repository through the record script and no other way (ruling 12) — **and because a reporter has a shell, the check vets first**: every file of the run that is written once and in no commit yet is held to what that script would have written, and one that is not **is no report** — it is moved out of the tree, the check names it (`aside`), and its reporter has left none (→ The record step, *What is vetted, and where*). **Whether a report is there is what that check says, never what an agent returned**: a result counts only with its report, a file nobody launched stops the stage, and a launched reporter that left none is taken off the list — what it was launched for is recorded as not done (→ *How an agent can end*, below).
6. **Triage and verify-real**, alternating (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks).
7. **The record step** (→ The record step): the reports checked, what the candidate's gate showed red, **one result per item the round ran** — for a held check the file the tool kept its verdict in, and no word — the ledger rows, the round's triage record, the round's facts — written as one batch; then the full gate on the tree with them, held by the tool; then one commit on the loop branch, pushed.
8. **The state, read back**, and `next` returned with it.

**How an agent can end, and what the stage does then** (2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *What becomes of an agent that does not report*). An agent returns its result, halts with a report, halts without one, returns nothing after its retries, or writes its report and dies. The harness's header owns the table (*HOW AN AGENT CAN END, AND WHAT FOLLOWS*); what the orchestrator meets:

| The agent | does not report — however | and the stage |
|---|---|---|
| the first preflight · the scope step · triage | no result, or a result with no report | **halts**, naming it: nothing can be driven, selected or graded without it. The attempt is counted. **A first preflight or a scope step that returned and left no *report* is found at the report check, which runs after the instruments** (`stood-on`, below) |
| the second preflight (the trial image, the checks the scope selected) | no result, or an image that is not verified | **goes on**: the trial arms and those checks are *void* with that reason, and no trial arm is launched. One that returned a verified image and left no report halts the stage at the report check, after its arms ran (`stood-on`) |
| a step of an item's chain | no result, a halt, or no report | **goes on**: that item is *void* with the step and the reason, the rest of its chain is not launched, and `next` then asks for its re-run. A finding it did return with its report is triaged. |
| **the cross-model pass** | *any* of these, or its tool does not answer | **goes on, with the item's own passes**: that pass is recorded as not having run (`cross-model-void`) and nothing else follows — the item is never void for it |
| a verifier | no verdict, a verdict for another finding, or no report | **goes on**: the finding stays *unverified*, and the record script counts the pass (→ *A finding the triage cannot settle*) |
| an advocate · the independent drive | no case, no drive, or a report missing | **goes on**: half a fork is no fork — nothing of it is recorded, the finding stays unverified, and the next triage verifies it and drives the fork again |

The return's `counts.voided` names the void items and `unverified` each finding left without a verdict, with why.

**A return that lacks a field the stage needs voids what its reporter was launched for — and so does a hash that came back wrong** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *An absent field voids its item*; the second repair's `X1`). The fields below are **optional in every schema of a stage, and stay so**: a return that omits a field its schema requires does not come back partial — the call fails, three whole runs of the agent, and counts toward the breaker (→ The runtime probes). So the harness is handed the return without the field, and the absence is **that reporter's fact, never the stage's**: the row above for that reporter, with a reason that names the field and the reporter. The harness holds the cells as one table (`NEEDS`), per role that drives:

| The role | lacks | and then |
|---|---|---|
| a step of an item's chain — a reviewer, a driver | `asserted_sha256` | its item is **void**, the rest of its chain is not launched, `next` asks for its re-run. **What it found is triaged all the same**: a finding's text and repro do not rest on the hash, and whatever triage grades a break is driven again by a verifier on the binary *it* asserts |
| a verifier | `verdict`, or `asserted_sha256` | the finding stays **unverified**; what the verifier left open is triaged all the same |
| an advocate · the independent drive | `asserted_sha256` | **no fork is on record** and the finding stays unverified; the proposal of a case argued on a binary nobody asserted is not driven |
| a verifier, with `confirmed` | `regression`, or the hash of the previous release's binary (`ran_on.previous`) — or returns another hash for that binary | **the verdict stands, and its regression fact is unknown** (the second repair's `X1b`): the harness sends the record `confirmed` with `regression` **absent** — it sends the field only when it is a boolean *and* the previous binary's hash came back and is the one handed over. The record's cell is `-`, never `no`; the finding is confirmed, **not** counted unverified, and is routed by its door as a confirmed non-regression is; its fork, if it is contested, is driven and recorded with it. The state names it for as long as it is not recorded (`regression_unknown`), and every return counts it |

**A hash that is returned and is not the binary's is the same cell as one that is not returned** — until `X1` it halted the stage. *The argument:* a definition tells a driving agent to **halt** where the binary it is handed does not hash to its line, and that halt voids its item and nothing else; the comparison the harness makes is that halt, made for an agent that did not make it — so the same fact costs the same whether the agent noticed. And the fact is that agent's alone: every other agent's evidence is held to its own assertion, the binary is the step tool's and placed read-only, and a binary swapped under a whole stage shows as every later reporter void, on record and by name, where a halt records nothing and runs every instrument again to meet the same agent. Nothing that agent drove is recorded as evidence either way. The return names who did not assert the candidate's binary (`unasserted`: the reporter, and the hash it returned or `null`), beside `counts.voided`; the reason on record carries both hashes. Every optional field of a driving role's return that is *not* in the table has its reason in the harness fence (`NOT_NEEDED`) — one of them a declared default: a verifier's `contested` left out reads as *not contested*, which an omission cannot be told from.

**What still halts a stage once an instrument is launched** — the whole list, as the harness holds it (`HALTS_AFTER`): from the stage's first held check on, and in the lap that finishes a triage from its triage on, a halt names its row, and the fence is red on one that does not. **Every other ending of every agent voids what that agent was launched for, and the stage reaches its record.**

| The row | The stage halts when | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| `names` | a reporter name cannot be made for a step of an item the round runs: the item's id is too long | the held checks ran; the test set is corrected at an opening |
| `report-check` | the report check after the instruments could not be read — its step died or refused, or the breaker is tripped — or it names a file no launched reporter wrote | the instruments ran, nothing is recorded, the attempt is counted |
| `scope-aside` | the round's scope was set aside by that check: the file at its path is not what the record script would have written | the same |
| `stood-on` | a reporter the stage stands on left no report: the attempt's marker, the first preflight, the scope step, a second preflight that provided, the preflight of a finishing lap | the same — found after the instruments, not before them (the re-review's `R-M3`, a row) |
| `triage` | **any** ending of triage but its grades with its report — no result, a halt, counts that do not balance, a key that is none or given twice, no report — and, inside a pass, a verifier's name that cannot be made and a report check that could not be read | the same, **and the next attempt can halt the same way**: findings live in returns until the batch (the plan review's `B4`, a row) |
| `record` | the record step: the executor applied nothing or its batch was refused, the record's gate left no verdict, or the commit step refused | the batch stays applied where it was; the next invocation finishes it (→ The record step). A batch refused for one entry's text loses the stage (`B4`) |
| `push` | the push of the record's commit failed or was refused | nothing: the record is committed, and the next invocation's first read makes the push that is owed |
| `read-back` | the state could not be read back once the record was committed and pushed | nothing: the next invocation reads it |

*Not on the list, and not a void either:* two agents of one invocation that die under different labels trip the rate-limit breaker, and every later call returns nothing — the stage then halts at its next step, which is `report-check` or `triage` above (the re-review's `R-M2`, a row).

**What the orchestrator may pass**, beside the three required arguments — the harness's header, *Usage*, is the home of each:

- **`scope`** — the human widens or narrows a round: everything, a commit range, or named doors. Absent, the run's default scope applies. A round's scope is written once, so this is decided before the invocation. **The harness does not itself hold it**: the argument is validated, put into the scope step's prompt and used nowhere else, and nothing compares the scope that step writes with it. The bound is the scope role's own rule — named doors are included as named, the rest excluded — so a door the step writes in other words than the invocation names leaves its items unselected, which `never_selected` then shows (a declared bound).
- **`clause`** — required exactly when the state asks for a re-run (`next: retest`): the first clause its `retest` names. The invocation runs the items of that clause the state lists as due, **as one more attempt inside the round that selected them** — it begins no round and resolves no scope. A `clause` the state does not ask for, and a `retest` answered without it, are refused before any agent runs.
- **`crossModel`** — the items whose source pass is *also* read by a model of another family, **named one by one**. No value means every item; with none named, no stage calls, names or needs that tool, and a machine without it runs the whole workflow. By the human's ruling a named item's pass that could not run — the tool does not answer, or its agent dies, halts or leaves no report — is void for that pass only and fails nothing else: the item's own source pass runs regardless, and the round's record says which items got one and which were named and got none. It is a human-approved suggestion and never auto-run ([milestone-planning-workflow.md](milestone-planning-workflow.md) → the loop's Review step); naming the item is the approval. *Limited and rare, per item, never required* — the human's ruling ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The stabilization harness, as built*).
- **`stopAfter`** — return after a named step, for tuning. **After `state` the invocation only looks**: its first read finishes nothing — no batch is gated or committed, no push is made, no head moves — and what the next invocation would finish first is returned as `owed`. After any later step the stopped stage runs no record step of its own: what it wrote through the record script stays on disk — the attempt's marker, the reports written so far and the round's scope — and the stopped attempt counts as one that reached no record (→ **Halt and resume**). **But an invocation that stops after a later step reconciles first, as every invocation does**: it makes a push that is owed, and a batch an earlier invocation left applied is gated, committed and pushed by it — it then returns `recorded` and starts nothing.

**What comes back.** `status: 'triaged'` with the round, the candidate and its binary's hash, the record's commit, `counts`, `forks`, `unverified`, `unasserted`, `held`, `scope`, what forbids closing, `next` — and, in every return, `named` and `arrived` (→ Reading the next step, *What the harness reads*). **A return holds counts and ids, never a text of the record**: `counts.blockers` and `counts.for_the_human` are how many, and the lists themselves — the human's list, the blockers, the untriaged rows — lie in the state document's file, which `named.document` gives by its path and its sha256 and which a subagent reads for the orchestrator. What the orchestrator reads beside `next`: `counts.voided`, the items that did not run to their end; `unasserted`, who drove and did not assert the candidate's binary; `held`, how each held check ended — the tool's word, its job, and the file its output is in; `named.never_selected` and `named.uncovered`, what the record script computes and nothing can hold (→ *The derivation's inputs are held*, below); and `scope.uncovered` and `scope.reached_but_excluded`, the scope step's own reading — the doors of the round's test set that no item reaches, and the doors its derivation reaches that a scope set by the human leaves out. None of these is a finding. `reached_but_excluded` is in no table of the run: it is in this return and in the scope step's report, and is what the human needs at the next stop.

**Evidence belongs to an item of the test set, and a clause's status is derived** (2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *Evidence belongs to a test-set item*). A stage records **one result per item it ran** — in the round whose scope selected the item: which attempt, on which commit, and green, void with why, or red — and nothing about a clause. The record script derives each clause's status from those rows and renders the clause table from it; the rule is its header's (*A CLAUSE'S STATUS IS DERIVED*), and in three lines:

- **an item is owed by the latest tested round whose scope selects it, and an item no scope has selected owes nothing** — that is the scoped design (ruling 10), not a gap;
- **a clause is green only when every item it is owed has a green run that is still current** — a hunt's while no landed fix reaches its doors, which the round after a fix stage says by its scope; a check's only on the present candidate;
- **it is void while such an item is void, never ran to its end, or is no longer current** — and red while one is red.

A result says how an item *ran*, never what it found: what a hunting instrument found is the ledger's, and forbids closing there. So a later round that ran the other items of a clause cannot turn an instrument green that never ran to its end — the first of the review's serious findings.

**The derivation's inputs are held where they are written, and what cannot be held is named** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The derivation's inputs are held where they are written*; the second repair's `K4`). A status computed from an input nobody held is a wrong `close`, so three things are said apart, because they differ in kind. **Refused at the write.** *An item judges a clause of the census*: `item-set` refuses a row whose clause the census does not name, and `run-set` refuses — whole — a census that does not name a clause an item already judges, so the two typings of a clause at an opening cannot disagree in either order of writing; a test set bent by hand afterwards is refused as corrupt when the state is read. *And what the derivation reads of an item stands once it counts*: from the first round whose results hold a run of the item, or the first tested round whose scope selects it, its clause, when it runs, its doors and its registries are refused any change — a row that moved one would take a void run, or what a round owes, out of every clause's status. The legitimate ways to change an instrument all remain, and each is a write the script takes: its brief and its kind are corrected at any time, by a row that names those four as they stand; a row nothing has run and nothing owes is replaced whole; an instrument that covers something else is a **new item under its own id**, which the rounds that reach it then owe a run; and removing what a round owes is no write at all — a run whose test set changes is opened again. **Named in the state.** `never_selected` — every in-scope item that no *tested* round's scope has selected, by id — and `uncovered` — every door a round's scope *included* that no item of the test set names, by the door or by its registry, with the rounds whose test set held it. The record script computes both on every read. **A declared bound — neither list forbids anything.** A round's doors are derived afresh by its scope step, so there is no list an item's door could be held to, and a door mistyped by one letter reads exactly as a door the round's change did not reach: the item is never selected, owes nothing, and where another item of its clause is green **the state answers `close` without it** (the plan review's `B10`, driven; pinned as that answer by the script's suite). The script cannot tell a mistyped door from an item the scoped design rightly leaves out — an item no change of the run ever reaches, a door no instrument was built for — so it names both and decides neither; an item a later round leaves out was selected once and is not named. The bound's reach is therefore exactly **whoever reads the two names**: at every stop, and at the close, beside the evidence. A name that should not be there has its exit — the item has no run and no round owes it, so `item-set` corrects its door, and the tested round that included that door then owes it a run, which `next` asks for as `retest`. **Every return of a stage names both** — `named.never_selected`, the ids, and `named.uncovered`, how many doors of the round in hand no item names, with the state document's file for the names — at every stop and at the close (→ Reading the next step, *What the harness reads*). *Whose the bound is:* the human's to accept, the first of the declared bounds of the first run (→ Open questions and declared bounds). Whether a never-selected in-scope item should instead forbid closing until a recorded ruling says otherwise is his to rule too, and needs a form the record does not have — a ruling per item ([decisions-pending.md](decisions-pending.md) → `wf-never-selected`).

## Reading the next step

`next` is computed by the record script from the ledger, the clause table, the rounds' records and the run's facts. **When each value holds, and the order in which they are tried, is the script's** — its header, *THE NEXT STEP* and *THE STOP* — and is held to a truth table by its suite. What follows is only what the orchestrator does.

| `next` | The orchestrator |
|---|---|
| `not-ready` | finishes the opening: the state's `not_ready` names what is owed (→ Opening a run). No stage starts. |
| `test` | starts `test`. There is no round yet, the latest round's `test` never reached its record, or a round's fix stage is over — landed, a part landed, or dropped — and what it left is tested by nobody. |
| `triage` | invokes the stage whose `position` carries `triage: true` — with no `scope`, `clause` or `crossModel`. It grades, verifies and records the findings the state lists as `untriaged`, and runs no instrument. **This step is taken once per finding**: a finding that the round's triage has left without a grade or a verdict a second time is no longer `untriaged` — it is on `human_list`, and the step is `rule`. |
| `rule` | takes it to the human — three lists, each naming why. The return's `rule` gives how many findings and names the clauses and the stage; the findings themselves a subagent reads from the state document's file. The findings of `human_list` (found outside the test set · in need of a bound · **a fork** · **still ungraded or unverified after the one more triage that is automatic**), the clauses of `human_clauses` (still not green after the one re-run), and the stage of `human_stages` (**it left reports and no record twice in a row**). What the human may answer to each is under → Triage, the human's list and the forks. |
| `fix` | starts `fix` — at once, when `test` left nothing to rule on (ruling 9). |
| `retest` | starts `test` with `clause` set to the **first** clause the state's `retest` names. **A re-run is an attempt inside the round that selected the item, never a round of its own** — over that round's doors and **on that round's candidate, never the branch's tip**: it counts against no bound, and the state's `position.test.rerun` names the round, the items and the attempt each one's run will be. One invocation runs the due items of one clause in one round; the next state names the rest. An invocation without `clause` is refused. |
| `unsettled` | takes it to the human. Nothing is open, and a clause is void while **no item of the test set judges it** — there is no instrument to run — or while **items judge it and no tested round's scope has selected one**: it has no run on record, and owes none. The rulings name no step. The state's `unsettled` names the clause and which of the two it is. The ways out are under → Close. |
| `close` | closes the run (→ Close) — an answer that closes nothing until the repair is recorded. The return carries each clause's evidence and the close's first git step. |
| `stop` | returns to the human, with `stop.why` — after every round, at the bound across rounds, or at the bound on a round's fix cycles — and `stop.then`, the step that follows the go (→ Bounds and exits). |

**No step repeats without a count on record and an exit to the human** (2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *Every loop has a counter on record*). Four values are steps the orchestrator takes without asking — `test`, `triage`, `fix`, `retest` — and each is bounded by a number the record script counts: the rounds that have a fix stage, the triage records that left a finding unsettled, the fix cycles of a round, the runs of an item in its round — and, for a stage that halts before its record, its attempts that left reports and nothing else. Where a count is spent the step is `rule` or `stop`, and it becomes a stage's step again only by a fact the human's ruling writes. The pairs, and what bounds each, are the script's header (*EVERY LOOP HAS A COUNTER ON RECORD*).

**A stage can answer with something other than a finished stage**, and each has one meaning:

- `refused` — nothing ran beyond, at most, two reads. The arguments were malformed; or the stage is not fit for use and refuses to start (`not_fit`, with the build's record — the `fix` stage, until its repair and re-review are recorded; → What is built, and what is not); or the state's `position` refuses this stage now, in one word with one sentence of what to do instead — among them `attempts-spent`, where the stage has left reports and no record twice in a row and the next attempt is the human's to grant; that refusal carries `next`.
- `halted` — a step did not do what the stage needs. Committed work stands (→ Halt and resume). Three halts are not faults but the human's turn: `halted.phase` is `bound`, `fork`, or `fix` where no fixer fixed anything (→ Bounds and exits · Triage, the human's list and the forks · A round's `fix`).
- `ruled` — an invocation that only carried rulings recorded them and started nothing.
- `recorded` — the invocation found a batch that an earlier record step had applied and not committed, ran the gate on it again, committed and pushed it, and did nothing else (→ The record step). `next` says what follows.
- `landed` · `dropped` — how a `fix` stage ended a round. `cycle` · `nothing-to-fix` — a `fix` stage that had nothing it could do; `next` says what is owed.
- `stopped` — a `stopAfter` return. After `state` the invocation only looked: it finished nothing, and `owed` names what the next one finishes first.

**A line holds what the harness acts on, and nothing else — and since `K3` it is the only line a stage reads** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *A line holds what the harness acts on*; the second repair plan's `K9`). The relay probe observed that a line relayed through an agent never arrives byte for byte once it holds one character outside ASCII — the tool prints it as an escape, the agent's return decodes it, and the hash fails — and that from about 80 KB it does not arrive at all: so **the first state read of a real stage failed as the harness stood**. Every act of [`dev/stabilize-step`](../dev/stabilize-step) therefore prints, when asked (`--digest <scratch>`), a **digest** in place of its line: ids, words of a fixed list, counts, hashes and paths of plain segments, each field of a shape the tool declares, a value that does not fit its shape left out and named (`unfit`) — printable ASCII with no escape in it, held to its hash as every line is. What it leaves out is in a file it names by its path under that root and by its sha256: the state document for the state, and for every other act the line the act prints unasked, a refusal's halt report in it. **The state's digest grows with the test set and with nothing else** — about 135 bytes an item; 6.1 KB at 60 ledger rows, 150 doors on each side of the scope, 30 items and a human's list of 20, where the document is 92 KB — and what an agent needs of the rest it reads by key from the record script: an item's row with its brief (`item`), the doors of a round's test set an item covers (`item-doors`, for the round asked — a re-run's is not the latest), the untriaged rows (`untriaged`), a pending batch's subject (`pending`). The header of the step tool, *THE DIGEST*, has every field. Without the flag every line is what it was: the tool's own suite, the canary's setup and a reader at a terminal ask for it so, and a digest's file *is* that line. What shows that a digest is relayed whole is no suite's to show — the suites model the relay as the decoding that was observed — but the relay probe's, run again on 2026-10-07: whole, twelve of twelve ([The runtime probes](#the-runtime-probes)).

**What the harness reads, and what an agent resolves from the files** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *What the harness reads, changed once*; the second repair plan's `K3`). The harness's header owns it (*WHAT THIS SCRIPT READS*, *HOW A STEP CAN END*); what the orchestrator and a stage's agents meet:

- **Every step of a stage asks for its digest** (`--digest <the invocation's scratch root>`), and the harness reads a step's line only as one: it holds to its hash, is printable ASCII with no backslash, names what did not fit and the file with the rest, and has nothing struck out. The line the tool prints unasked — the state document in it — is refused even where it hashes, and nothing of a line that is not read is passed on.
- **No state document, no ledger row, no brief, no door list and no refusal's prose passes through the harness.** An agent that needs one is handed **the read**, and runs it: `dev/stabilize-record item --run <run> --item <item>` for its brief (a unit's prompt, the preflight's list of checks, the cross-model pass), `dev/stabilize-record item-doors --run <run> --round <N> --item <item>` for its door list — `N` the round in hand, a re-run's from its position — `dev/stabilize-record untriaged --run <run>` for the rows triage is handed, which the harness holds to their count, and `dev/stabilize-record pending --run <run>` for a pending batch's subject. What an agent returns of its own judgment — a finding, a grade, a verdict, a scope's derivation, its own halt report — is passed on as it came.
- **A return holds what the digest holds — words, ids and counts — and names the file for the rest.** `named.document` is the state document's file, by its path and its sha256: the human's list, the blockers and the untriaged rows are *how many* in the return and lists there, and a `next` the script has no sentence for comes back with what the digest holds of the state and that file, never the document. **A subagent reads the file for the orchestrator.** `named` also carries what nothing can hold — `never_selected`, the in-scope items no tested round has selected, and `uncovered`, how many doors of the round no item names (the file names them) — and `regression_unknown`, how many findings are confirmed with their regression fact unknown and not yet recorded (the digest lists their keys, the file says which) — in every return, at every stop and at the close (→ A round's `test`, *The derivation's inputs are held*). `arrived` says what the invocation's first read met, finished and left owed.
- **A step's refusal is its word.** A halt carries `refused`, the word; `whose`, whose the state is; the harness's own sentence of what leaves it — it has a row for every word the step tool refuses with, held to the tool's by the fence — and `step`, the file the tool's own line lies in, its halt report in it. Three words are nobody's to take to the human: `locked` (the orchestrator removes git's lock once no git process runs, and invokes again), `head-moved` (somebody committed while the stage ran: invoke again) and `foreign-commit` (**its author's** — the commit is pushed by name, `git push origin <branch>`, and the stage invoked again).
- **Every invocation reconciles first, and the one that only looks does not.** The first read finishes a killed write, settles a batch whose commit was made and **makes the push that is owed** — so *the same args again* after a push that failed is true; a batch that is applied and not committed is gated and committed by that invocation, which starts nothing else. Under `stopAfter: 'state'` the read only looks (`--look`): nothing is finished, no head moves, and the return's `owed` names what the next invocation finishes. A push the tool refused as `unvetted` is the human's and is **never** the push that is owed; the same ruling sent twice is `refused` (`no-change`) and leaves no batch; and a record's commit refused as `unvetted` has taken its batch back already, so the halt asks for no discard. Where a halt does name the act that takes a batch back, it is `dev/stabilize-step discard --branch <branch> --run-dir <run dir>`, which asks git first.
- **A round tests one candidate, and every result of it names that commit** (the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-10-07 on the core review's `F3`). A round a `test` stage begins takes the branch's tip as its candidate. A re-run is an attempt inside a round that has one, with the round's record commits on top of it: it builds the candidate from its commit, hands every driving agent that binary and names the candidate in every result — the tip is named to the record's commit step alone, which is held to it. **And the record script holds it at the write**: once a round has its candidate, `result-set` takes that commit and refuses any other (`bad-value`), alone and in a batch — a re-run on the round's own candidate is taken wherever its record commits have moved the tip. **A check the preflight runs reads the working tree, which in a re-run is not the candidate: it is not asked for there and is recorded void with that reason** — never run on the tip and recorded for a commit it did not run on. So a check that did not run in its round's `test` stage is not run again inside that round, and its clause is the human's after the re-run. **That is accepted as built, as a declared bound for the first run** (the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-10-07): the tip differs from the candidate only by record commits, but proving that is the path rule's job, which is the `fix` half's repair — until then a check that is void in its round is settled by the next round, whose candidate is the tip, and such a round ends at `rule`, not `close`. A held check that names its commits is where that ends: the regression set is held again in a re-run, on the round's candidate. A held gate is not — it reads the working tree — and is recorded **void by a word** there (`off-tree`): a run that counts, after which its clause is the human's as a check's is (→ *What a stage's agent types, and how a long command is held*).
- **A held command is read as the tool's words.** The harness reads what a `hold-start` or `hold-wait` step says — `running`; `done`, as the tool's verdict `green`, `red` or `void` with the file the tool kept it in and the facts of its kind; `dead` — and nothing of it is an agent's reading: a binary's path, hash and version, a gate's verdict and a held check's result are each read off that line.

**A long command is started, waited for and judged by the tool** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *A long command is started, waited for and judged by the tool*; the second repair plan's `K10`). The gate takes about ten minutes and the regression set about thirty-five, and an agent's shell lets a call run two. Until this task a prompt told an agent to start such a command *in the background* and wait *in slices*, and the agent composed the waiting shell itself — a command no permission rule can name, at which the hold probe's agent stopped on a prompt nobody was watching (the human's ruling of 2026-10-07: *there should be tooling for this*). So [`dev/stabilize-step`](../dev/stabilize-step) holds it, in plain calls:

- **`hold-start --scratch <scratch> --name <name> --kind <kind> …` starts the command and returns at once.** The command is the tool's own, built from the kind and what the kind takes, never from a caller's text: `gate` (`dev/gate --keep-going`), `regression` (`dev/regression-set run` over two commits and the list), `build` (the `jigc` binary of a commit — unpacked from `git archive`, built in a target directory of its own, written once and read-only to a path under the scratch root) and `probe` (the hold probe's command). It runs in a session of its own, so it outlives the call and the agent that made it, and its whole output lies in a file **the tool names**, `<scratch>/hold/<name>/output` — no redirect. **A start is idempotent by name**: asked again it answers with the job that is there and starts nothing; the same name with other arguments is refused (`taken`).
- **`hold-wait --scratch <scratch> --name <name>` answers within a slice of 90 seconds** — the tool's number, inside the two minutes of an agent's shell, and a caller may only shorten it — with `running`, `done` or `dead`. One agent waits by asking again; because the command outlives every caller another agent can take the wait over, and two waiters at once are both answered.
- **When the command has run to its end the tool, and no agent, reads its verdict** — out of the command's own output, once, kept as one hashed line (`<scratch>/hold/<name>/verdict.json`): a gate from its verdict line and its totals line, by the record script's own reader, so that a pre-check's output is no verdict; the regression set from its one line, held to the two commits and the list that were asked; a build from its own line and the binary's hash on the disk. `green`, `red`, or `void` with why — and a verdict the command's exit status does not bear out is void. **A job whose process is gone without its end on record is `dead`**: named, never `done`, whatever its output holds, and not started again under its name.

**A scripted check is a kind of item, and its result is the tool's verdict.** An item of kind `held-<id>` — `held-regression`, `held-gate` — is a check that is one held command. `dev/stabilize-record result-set` takes for it a row that names the item and the verdict's file, and nothing else: **no outcome word is taken from a caller**, and the file is that tool's line, of that kind, on the call's commit, or the call is refused. The verdict reaches the record as a file written once, `r<N>/checks/<item>.a<attempt>.json` — for the regression set the two commits, the list's hash and what was excluded are in it — a red one is filed as a finding like every red check, and a result whose verdict file is gone or says another word is refused where the state is read. **The stage starts each one itself** — after the candidate's gate, one after another — and a `held-gate` is answered from the candidate's own gate, which is not run twice.

**What a stage's agent types, and how a long command is held** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *What the harness tells an agent to run*; the second repair plan's `K11`; the human's ruling of 2026-10-07 that no step of a stage may need his permission). **Every command a prompt of the harness names is ONE plain invocation of one of three committed tools** — `dev/stabilize-step`, `dev/stabilize-record`, and in a probe `dev/stabilize-probe`: no pipe, no redirect, no `&&`, no `;`, no `$(…)`, no loop, no `sleep`, no `nohup`, no `&`, no `cd`, no variable set in front of it, and no other program. The gate, the regression set, cargo, git and a hash are reached *through* the step tool's acts and never typed by an agent. One function of the harness renders such a command, a fence composes every prompt the harness can compose and holds each command in it to that shape (`stabilize_harness_fence`, arm (s)). **No allow rule for the three tools is committed yet.** Where the rules live — the committed settings, which travel into every clone, the canary's among them, or the human's own, which do not — is his question, a permission being nobody else's to grant; until it is answered, the session's permission mode decides whether one of these plain calls asks (→ Open questions and declared bounds, *Open, the human's to rule*).

- **A long command is held by two steps, each one plain call.** One step *starts* it (`hold-start`): the tool starts the command in a session of its own and answers at once. Another step *asks after it* (`hold-wait`): the tool answers within its own minute and a half, and its agent **runs that same call again for as long as the tool says `running`** — it composes no shell around it and keeps no time of its own. Then the tool, and no agent, reads the verdict. A stage holds this way: the candidate's binary and the previous release's, the candidate's gate, each held check, and every record's gate.
- **A text reaches its writer from a file.** A report, the round's scope and a record's batch are each written by the agent **with its file tool**, to the path under the scratch root that its prompt names, and handed over by one plain call that names that path (`report … --from`, `scope-set … --from`, `apply … --from … --sha256`): the writer holds a batch to the hash the harness composed, and refuses another. So **every role that reports has a tool that writes a file**: the human's ruling of 2026-10-08 gave one to the six definitions that had only a shell ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The agent definitions follow the harness, and six roles get a tool that writes a file*). Whether a file written with it under a scratch root outside the repository asks for a permission of its own is not observed yet — the `payload` probe, run again, shows it.
- **How a held command can end, and what follows** — one table, in the harness (`HELD_TAKES`):

| What is held | green | red | void | dead | still running, or nobody left to ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a binary's build | the binary: its path, hash and version are the tool's | halt | halt | halt | halt, naming the job |
| the candidate's gate | a fact of the round, from the file the tool kept | the same: what is red is on record | halt — nothing could be recorded against it | halt | halt, naming the job |
| a held check | its result is the verdict's file | the same; the record script files the finding | the same: a void run, with the tool's reason | **void by a word**: the stage goes on and records `{item, outcome: void, reason}` — the reason `dead:<job>`, a run that counts — and the state asks for its re-run where it owes one | the same, by `running:<job>`; a start the tool refused, by the tool's own word (`taken:<job>`); and the return names the job |
| a record's gate | the one commit, held to the candidate's gate | the same | halt: nothing is committed, the batch stays applied | the same halt | the same halt, naming the job |

- **Who takes a wait over.** A held command outlives every agent. A *start* whose agent died is asked for again as its one command, and the tool answers with the job that is there — nothing starts twice. A step that *asks* and whose agent died is asked for again, and its one command is the wait, never the start: the wait is taken over, and the command ran once. An agent whose turn ended while the tool still said `running` is followed by the next, three in turn; then the stage halts and names the job, with the one call that answers once it is over — `dev/stabilize-step hold-wait --scratch <scratch> --name <name>` — and is invoked again after that: the next attempt names and starts its own command, and takes nothing over from the last.
- **A name is one command's.** A stage's held commands are named by its round and its attempt, which the record hands out once. A record's gate that no attempt names — a pending batch, the human's rulings — is named in sequence: where the first try of a start is answered by a job that was there already, that job is an earlier invocation's, a verdict of the tree as it stood then, and the next name is asked.
- **What is not a plain call, and why** — each a named exception of the fence, red if it stops being real: *the independent drive of an advocate's proposal* clones, patches and builds by a shell of its own, because a patched tree is no commit and the tool builds a commit and nothing else (the hash it asserts is asked of the tool); *the cross-model pass*, launched only for an item an invocation names, runs a tool of another family that the commit holds no wrapper for; and *the re-cut of a part* is the `fix` half's list of git commands, about to go. **What a role runs of its own craft is named by no prompt and held by no fence**: a reviewer's rig, a driver's commands on the binary, the preflight's read of CI, the tarball install, the trial image. A permission prompt can appear in any of those.
- **A held check that left no verdict is void by a word** (the commit that finished `X1b`; the record script's side is `afcde397`). `void`, and only `void`, is the one word a caller may say of a held check; its reason has a declared shape — `<word>` or `<word>:<key>`, a slug and the held job's name — which the harness composes from what it already reads and the fence holds to the record script's pattern: `off-tree` for a held gate in a re-run, the tool's own word where it refused the start, and else how the job stood when the stage stopped asking (`dead`, `running`, `unread`). It is a run on record, so the state leaves `retest`: after a round whose gate item was red, the re-run records the gate void and the clause is the human's — settled by the next round, whose candidate is the tip. *What it costs:* such a re-run still runs a preflight and builds the candidate's binary to record one row.
- **Two bounds.** *A held gate is not held again inside its round* — it reads the working tree, which is no longer the round's candidate. *A command left running is nobody's to end*: the tool kills nothing, two gates at once contend for one lock, and a held check recorded void as `running` may still end green under its name, read by nobody.

## Triage, the human's list and the forks

**Every finding is graded, and nothing leaves the list unseen** (ruling 5). Triage grades every finding a stage produced — a lead, and every entry an agent *left open*, included — and keys each to one ledger row for the life of the run. An independent verifier re-drives everything graded as breaking or unclear, with a brief to refute it, and establishes on the previous release's binary whether it is a regression. The rule is the completion workflow's verify-real ([milestone-completion-workflow.md](milestone-completion-workflow.md) → The loop, step 2); what this workflow adds is who may decide what:

- **Whether a finding is inside the round's test set is computed** — its door, by exact text, against the round's committed door list. No agent supplies it.
- **Whether it is a regression is a fact** — green on the previous release's binary, red on the candidate.
- **A grade of *out of scope* must cite a declared bound** on the run's list; one that cannot goes to the human.
- **What an agent leaves open is triaged like any finding**, never queued for a fixer on its own. So triage and verification alternate — as many times as the harness's `VERIFY_PASSES` says — and what is still unverified after that leaves the round's triage *unfinished*: `next` is `triage`, once (→ *A finding the triage cannot settle*, below).

The vocabulary of grades, verdicts and dispositions, and the route each combination takes, are the record script's (its header: `ledger-set`, *THE ROUTE*).

**What is fixed without asking, and what is the human's** (ruling 4). A verified finding **inside** the test set, and a **regression** wherever it was found, is routed to a fixer. A verified finding that breaks the condition but was found **outside** the test set is the human's, as a list, at the end of the round. Per item the human gives one of three rulings:

- **admit** it to this run — it is then fixed like any blocker;
- **declare a bound**, with its reach, where the human ruled it, and the test that pins it or the word *unpinned*;
- **record it for a later release**.

**How a ruling is recorded.** The orchestrator passes the human's rulings to the next `fix` invocation as `rulings`; one step records them before anything is fixed, and the same invocation goes on to fix what is then routed to a fixer. An item left unruled keeps `next` at `rule`, and nothing is fixed beside it. The shapes are the harness header's (*Usage* → `rulings`). **While the `fix` stage refuses to start, the same rulings go to an invocation of `test` that carries `rulings` and nothing else**: it records them on the loop branch — a ruling on a finding, a declared bound — and starts nothing; `next` then names the step. A ruling on a key the ledger does not hold is refused, and nothing is recorded.

**This step is written to be replaceable.** Its input is the state's `human_list`; its output is the `rulings` argument. An agent that took the list over would change neither — and would still not declare a bound, which stays the human's ruling. Whether and under what rule an agent takes it is deferred, with no trigger but the human's word ([decisions-pending.md](decisions-pending.md) → *Handing the ruling of the outside-the-test-set list to an agent*).

**A finding the triage cannot settle.** A finding a round's triage leaves without a grade, or without a verdict, **gets one more triage automatically** — the invocation that finishes the round's triage: it begins its attempt on record, has the step tool build the two binaries **from the commit the round tested** — which the round's record commits have moved the branch off — grades the ledger's rows that await it, sends them to a verifier once more, and records. No instrument runs. The passes are counted by the record script, in the round's own record, by the call that records a triage: no invocation counts them, and a record that does not name the finding counts against it too. **Left so a second time, it is the human's**, on `human_list` with the reason (`unverified-after-retry`, `ungraded-after-retry`), and the human may do one of two things: **rule it** — one of the three rulings above, recorded as any ruling is — or **grant one more triage**: `rulings: [{ reverify: '<key>' }]`, on either stage, with the loop branch checked out. Each grant allows one more pass and is on record as that pass. *This is the orchestrator's reading, by analogy with the human's rule for a clause, reported to him and standing unless overturned.*

**A stage that is the human's.** A stage that halts before its record leaves its reports, and the next invocation works the next attempt. **That happens once without the human**: the record script counts the attempts above the last one that reached its record, and after two in a row the step is `rule`, `human_stages` names the stage and why, and the stage is refused (`attempts-spent`). What the earlier attempts halted on is in their returns and reports; once the cause is dealt with the human grants one more attempt — `rulings: [{ again: 'test' }]` (or `'fix'`) — one grant, one attempt.

**A clause that is the human's.** An item that is not green is run again once, automatically (`retest`) — its second attempt in the round that selected it. Still not green after that, its clause is in `human_clauses`, and the **one** ruling with a recorded form is one more re-run: `rulings: [{ rerun: '<clause>' }]`, on either stage, with the loop branch checked out. Each grant allows one more attempt of the items that are spent, and is on record as that attempt. Dropping or replacing a clause or an item is no ruling inside a round — it changes the closing condition or the test set, which the opening fixed, and so is a new opening.

**A fork.** A fix that is contested — the verifier found the finding to argue against a settled decision — or that needs a new mechanism — a fixer halted on it — goes to the human with a `robust-advocate`'s case, **and the proposal is driven before the human sees it**: by the advocate, and then by an agent that is not its author ([milestone-planning-workflow.md](milestone-planning-workflow.md) → the loop's Settle step, the claim-driven rule). Forks come back in a stage's `forks`, each with the case and the independent drive; a `fix` stage that met one returns `halted` with `phase: 'fork'`, the round unlanded. The orchestrator brings each to the human (→ Orchestration). The human's answer is recorded as a ruling on that finding — admitted, with a note that reaches the fixer; a bound; or later.

**A contested fix is on record** (2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *What becomes of an agent that does not report*, the fork's row). The round's triage record holds the fork with the verdict that raised it — its kind, the verdict of the advocate's case, and whether the independent drive found what the advocate reported — and the state reads it back on the finding's row (`triage.fork`). **While it is unruled the finding is the human's, whatever its verdict and wherever it was found**: it is on `human_list` with `why: 'fork'`, `next` is `rule`, and never `fix` or `close`. The ruling is one of the three, written by the one step that records rulings; *admitted* makes it a fixer's, with the human's note. The case and the drive are two reports of the stage, named for the finding (`advocate-…-<key>`, `proposal-…-<key>`), so a session that lost the return reads the fork from the state and both from the run's directory. **A fork is whole or it is none**: where the advocate or the independent drive did not finish, nothing of it is recorded and the finding stays unverified. *Its bound:* the fork a **fixer** raises — a fix that needs a new mechanism — is still in the `fix` stage's return only; that stage refuses to start, and its fork's row is its repair's.

**A red deterministic check is a finding.** The rulings give a check three answers — green, red, void. **The call that records a red result files it**: one ledger row, under a key of the item's own (`red-<item>`), ungraded and open — so no caller can record a red check and file nothing. It is then graded by the next triage that runs, as a row seeded at the opening is — until then `next` is `triage` — and routed, ruled or fixed like any other finding. A check that is red again after its finding was fixed opens that finding again. *Its bound:* the stage that found the check red does not hand it to its own triage, so the finding waits for the invocation that finishes the round's triage — one more invocation, and one more build of the two binaries. The record that files the finding counts as one pass that left it ungraded; the first triage that grades it and cannot verify it counts its first pass without a verdict. **A candidate whose *gate* is red is recorded like any other** — what its gate showed red is a table of the round, and the stage's record commit is held to that table (→ The record step).

## The record step

**A stage's tables are written in one place** — at the end of a `test` stage, of a finished triage and of a fix cycle, and by the step that records the human's rulings — and in three acts (2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The record step, rebuilt*; the mechanisms are the headers of [`dev/stabilize-record`](../dev/stabilize-record) and [`dev/stabilize-step`](../dev/stabilize-step)):

1. **The batch.** The record's calls — the report check first, the ledger check last — are written to a file by the stage's executor, with its file tool, and applied from it as **one batch**, which the record script holds to the hash the harness composed: every table is written, or none is, and a check that does not hold refuses the batch. The record script then holds it as *applied and not committed*. An invocation that finds a batch applied already runs no executor.
2. **The gate.** The full gate runs on the tree with the records — the step tool's held `gate`, which is `dev/gate --keep-going`, like the candidate's — started by one step and asked after by another, and the tool keeps its whole output. A gate that leaves no verdict commits nothing: the batch stays applied, and the next invocation holds the gate again, under the next name.
3. **The commit.** One git step holds that output to the gate of the round's candidate and makes the one commit — every pending path of the run by name, under the subject the batch carries — **when nothing is red that the candidate's own gate did not already show red** (the human's ruling of 2026-10-06; the rule's home is [dev-workflow.md](dev-workflow.md) → Gate). On a green candidate that is: when the gate is green. **And what it stages, it vets before it commits** (below).

**What is vetted, and where** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *What a step publishes is vetted where it becomes permanent*; the second repair plan's `P2`). The record script checks a text where it *writes* it. But a reporter has a shell, so a file at a report's path is whatever its last writer left, and a commit is whoever made it: the text the script refused, written to the same path by a redirect, used to be committed by its name and pushed. So what a writer checks — the path, **no host path left** (the public placeholders themselves pass), the line that would redden the fence, a report's last line, and **the two scanners CI runs over a push** — is checked again by that script's own read, `dev/stabilize-record vet`, at the three places where content becomes permanent. Each has its exit:

| Boundary | What is vetted | A hit |
|---|---|---|
| **every report check** | every file of the run that is written once and in no commit: the reports of every attempt, each round's scope | **the file is no report**: it is moved out of the tree — under the invocation's scratch root (`<scratch>/refused`), which the harness hands the step; a direct caller that names none gets a directory the record script mints — the check names it (`aside`, with the word of what refused it), and its reporter has left none. The item it was launched for is void — its reason says that the file was set aside — or, for a reporter the stage stands on, the stage halts there; **a round's scope that was set aside halts the stage there too**: the round has no scope any more. An answer, not a refusal: the stage goes on |
| **the record's commit** | every path the commit adds, as staged; the subject; and the batch's own report checks, held to the disk again — a report such a check counted is still there, with the bytes it had | `unvetted`: **nothing is committed and the batch is taken back** — the step unstages what it staged, discards the batch, and moves a report or a scope that is no such file out of the tree. `git-state` then answers `ready`, and the stage invoked again works the next attempt. The instruments run again; it is reached only by a file that changed between the report check and the commit |
| **every push a step makes** — a stage's, a landing's, a carried record's | every commit that no branch of the remote holds yet: the two scanners over that range, as CI runs them; and for a commit that changes a file of an opened run, the writer's rules on each such file and no host path in its message | `unvetted`: **nothing is pushed, and it is the human's** — the commit is local, and rewriting it or taking it back is his to confirm. The refusal names the read that names every commit and place again and pushes nothing: `dev/stabilize-record vet --range -- <branch> --not <the commits the remote's heads stood at>`, spelled out by the refusal — what the remote lacks is asked of the remote (`git ls-remote --heads origin`), never read off a remote-tracking ref |

A hit is named by where it is, never by what matched. **A scanner that cannot run is a refusal at each of the three** (`did-not-run`), never a pass, and leaves everything as it was: the same step is asked again once the scanner runs. **What is not vetted:** a file outside a run's directory is held to the two scanners and to nothing else — the placeholder rule is the record's, and source code names `/tmp`; and **a push somebody makes by hand with plain git is outside the tool** — CI's scan is hard, and runs after it.

**A push names the commit that was vetted, and publishes nothing later** (2026-10-08 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The two halves of the core, joined and finished*, part 2; the focused review's `F1`, `F2` and `F6`). The tool has one push, in one helper, and `git-state`'s owed push, `push`, `land` and `carry` all make it: the remote is asked what it holds; the branch's commit is read once and the vet is of that commit, by its sha; both are asked again after the vet; and the push is `git push origin <sha>:refs/heads/<branch>` — the vetted object into the branch's own ref, never the branch by name, which is whatever it stands at when git reads it. What a push can refuse with, and what is then on the remote:

| Refusal | When | On the remote | What leaves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unvetted` | a commit of the range is not what the record script would have written, or a scanner names it | nothing new | **the human's**: the commit is local (the table above) |
| `remote-differs` | **while the range was vetted** the remote came to list other heads, or the branch came to stand at another commit | nothing new | the step asked again vets what the remote lacks then |
| `remote-differs` | **after the push** the remote does not stand at the vetted commit — another push's, since; or **the branch gained a commit while the vetted one was pushed** | the vetted commit, by its sha, and nothing later: the act names the commits that are **not** published | the push is owed: the next first read meets what landed — `foreign-commit` where it is no record's |
| `push-rejected` | the remote did not take the push — or **none was made, because a tag or another ref answers to the branch's name**: git reads a short name as a tag first, and a push of a sha is not one git refuses as ambiguous | nothing new | the orchestrator's; a tag of that name is the human's to delete |

*Not closed by any push without force:* git moves the remote forward from where its own handshake finds it, which nothing can hold to the position the vet saw.

**A record act is repeatable, and every invocation reconciles first** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *A record act is repeatable, and every invocation reconciles first*; the second repair plan's `P1`). A record is three commands — the batch applied, the commit step, the push — and a killed agent, a `git commit` that fails (signing is an ordinary reason) or a push the remote does not take leaves the run *between* two of them. Until this change each such state was one every later act refused, or one that read as finished: a record that was committed and not pushed was said by nothing, and the next round began on it. **Every such state now has a name and an answer, in one table that [`dev/stabilize-step`](../dev/stabilize-step) holds as data, prints (`dev/stabilize-step table`) and answers from.** The table is keyed by **act and phase**: an act is a step that changes git or the record, its phases are its sub-steps in order — for `record`: *applied* → *committed* → *settled* → *pushed* — and each phase has what says **from git and the journal, never from a flag alone**, that it is done, and the one step that resumes it. Reconciling is: for the open act, the first phase that is not done — finish it, or say who does. **`git-state`, the read every stage starts from, does it before it answers**, and names what it met (`found`), what it finished (`finished`) and what it left (`owed`); `record`, `discard` and the report check do the part of it they stand on.

| Arrival state | What is found | What the tool does | What leaves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wrong-branch` | the branch checked out is not the one the act names | refuses — **before any write, fetch or push** | the caller's: the branch is checked out, and the act asked for again |
| `git-lock` | git's own `index.lock` stands in the repository, and still does after two seconds | refuses (`locked`), naming the file; **the tool never removes a lock** | the orchestrator's: with no git process running there, the file is removed and the act asked for again |
| `interrupted-write` | a write of the record script was killed between its files — a batch half in place, or half taken back | finishes it: `dev/stabilize-record recover` | nothing left to do |
| `stray-temporaries` | that script's own temporaries, named by no journal: a write killed before its journal | undoes it: `recover` removes them, and the state is the one before that write | nothing left to do. *A temporary is no report*: the report check does not count one |
| `applied` | the journal holds a batch, its files are in the tree as written, and no commit holds it | reports it as `pending`, owed to the stage | the full gate on the tree, then `dev/stabilize-step record`; or `dev/stabilize-step discard` |
| `staged` | as `applied`, with paths of the record in the index: a commit step killed after its `git add`, or whose commit failed | as `applied`: the commit step admits its own staged lines | the same step, asked for again: it stages the paths again, vets them, commits |
| `altered` | a file of the applied batch is no longer what the batch wrote | refuses (`dirty`): **the human's** | `dev/stabilize-step discard --branch <branch> --run-dir <run dir>` takes the batch back, whatever a hand wrote since — the refusal spells the command — or the file is put back and the step asked for again |
| `head-moved` | the branch is not at the commit the stage began on (`record --head`) | refuses; nothing is committed or undone | the orchestrator's: the stage is invoked again, meets the commit that was made meanwhile, and gates the batch on the tree as it then stands |
| `committed` | the journal holds a batch and every file of it is in `HEAD` as written: the commit was made and the batch not forgotten | finishes it: `dev/stabilize-record settle`; `record` answers `recorded`, `discard` refuses (`committed`) | nothing left to do |
| `recorded` | the commit step is asked for again after it was done: no batch, the tree clean, and `HEAD` the one commit since the commit the stage began on | answers again what it answered then, from the answer it kept (below) — only where it is handed that commit (`--head`), which is what says that the one commit since is this record's; without it the answer is `no-batch` | nothing to leave |
| `unpushed` | the loop branch is ahead of its remote by commits that change only the run's directory | `git-state` makes **the push that is owed**, for the `test` stage, every commit of it vetted first; while a batch is applied it leaves the push to that record | nothing left to do |
| `unvetted` | a commit the push would publish is not what the record script would have written | refuses; nothing is pushed: **the human's** | `dev/stabilize-record vet --range -- <branch> --not <the commits the remote's heads stood at>`, as the refusal spells it, names every place again; taking a local commit back is his to confirm |
| `foreign-commit` | the loop branch is ahead of its remote by a commit that changes a path outside the run's directory, or by a merge | refuses; nothing is pushed — **neither the tool's nor the human's** | its author's: it is some task's commit, behind that task's own gate, pushed by name — `git push origin <branch>` — by its author or by the orchestrator for that task |
| `remote-ahead` | the pushed branch has commits the local one lacks | refuses: the human's | the human reconciles the two |
| `dirty` | anything else in the tree | refuses: the human's | the human reconciles it |

**Two end states, and no third.** A record step killed at any point between two of its own commands ends, within two reads, where the journal was not yet written, at **the state before the batch** — the tables as they were, the reports and the scope still pending, the attempt counted, nothing to push — and else at **the state of the record that was never interrupted**, pushed, with a clean tree. A test kills the three commands before every file operation of the batch's write and before every child command of the commit step and of the push, and holds each end to `state`, the tree, the journal and the remote ([`dev_stabilize_step`](../tooling-tests/dev_stabilize_step.rs), *a record step killed between its commands ends in one of two states*). **Where that ends:** a kill *inside* a git child. Its one common trace, the lock, has its row; that git moves a ref whole or not at all is relied on, not tested; anything else such a kill leaves is the last row.

**The commit step keeps its answer, so that a retry of it is answered** — a choice beyond the plan's text, made so that a second identical call is idempotent. Once its commit is made, and **before** the batch is settled, `record` writes what it answers — the commit, the batch's calls and checks, the gate check — to `<gate file>.recorded.json`, beside the file of the gate it was held to, outside the repository. So from the commit on there is no moment at which nothing says what was recorded: killed before that write the step finds its batch in the journal and in `HEAD` (`committed`); after the settling it finds this file (`recorded`). **The file is believed only where git agrees**: `HEAD` is the commit it names, that commit's one parent is the commit the stage began on, the tree is clean, and the calls and checks are the ones asked for. The tool's table lists it (`kept`), and a test kills the commit step before every one of its points — this write among them — and asks for it again: each time it answers `recorded` with the same commit.

**What the tool pushes for itself is a record's commit, and nothing beside one.** A tuning commit on the loop branch — a fix of the workflow between two rounds — is neither the tool's to push nor the human's to reconcile: it is its author's, behind its own gate, and **no `test` stage starts over one the remote lacks**. A record's own push says so too where it is asked as one (`push --run-dir`).

**The `fix` half's acts are rows of the same table** — `land`, `carry`, `open-round` are listed in it as that half's, with no phase: its repair adds their phases and their arrival states there, and the test that kills reads the table's print, so an act that gains phases gains its kills. Until then a `fix` act that is interrupted is what it was (→ Halt and resume).

**What the harness does with this** (the plan's task `K3`) — it starts every stage from this read's answer and returns what the read met (`arrived`); **it passes `--look`** where the invocation stops after `state`, which then finishes nothing and returns what is `owed`; **`--head`** to a record's commit step — the commit the stage began on, and after a record of the same invocation the head its push returned — so that a commit made on the loop branch while a stage ran is refused at the record (`head-moved`) and a commit step whose agent died after its command ran is asked for again and answers `recorded`; and **`--run-dir`** to the push of a record on the loop branch. Its halt sentences name **`dev/stabilize-step discard --branch <branch> --run-dir <run dir>`**, the act that asks git first — and name none where the batch is taken back already or was never written (→ Reading the next step, *What the harness reads*). The `fix` stage's own records are handed no `--head`: which head they are held to is its half's repair.

**What the orchestrator meets.**

- **A record whose gate is newly red is a halt that names what is new** — the step or the test that is red only with the records — with nothing committed and nothing undone. The batch stays applied, and that is no dirty tree: **the next invocation of the stage finds it, runs the gate on it again, commits and pushes it, and does nothing else**; it returns `recorded`. So a flaky test costs one more gate, never the round's instruments. Where the records themselves are what is red, the batch is taken back — the orchestrator's decision, a subagent's act: `dev/stabilize-step discard --branch <branch> --run-dir <run dir>` puts every table back as it was and keeps the reports and the round's scope — the cause is repaired, and the stage is run again as the next attempt. *(`dev/stabilize-record discard` reads no git, and takes a batch back whose commit is made: it is the script's own step, and never the one to call.)*
- **The same record sent twice is refused where it is applied** (`no-change`): a batch every file of which is what the run holds already — the same ruling, relayed again — writes nothing and leaves no journal, so it cannot stand in the way of the next record.
- **What is accepted as recorded is the commit step's own line**: the commit, the gate check that holds, and as many calls and checks as the harness composed, each check holding. A record step that says it recorded and shows none of that halts the stage.
- **What a stage wrote through the record script and no record step committed is pending, never a dirty tree**: its reports, the round's scope, an applied batch. The step that opens every stage asks the record script what is pending and holds the tree to exactly that; a table changed by hand, or a file of a batch changed after the batch wrote it, still halts.
- **A write of the record that was killed** — between two files, or before its journal — is finished by the next act of the step tool that reads the run's pending writes, `git-state` first among them, and named in that act's line. A direct read of the state (`dev/stabilize-record state`) still refuses across one, in one line that names what finishes it (`dev/stabilize-record recover --run <run>`).

**Both gates keep going** ([dev-workflow.md](dev-workflow.md) → Gate, *`dev/gate --keep-going`*). A plain gate stops at its first red stage, so under a candidate that was red early neither gate ran what lay behind the stop, and "nothing newly red" was judged on what ran. The candidate's gate and the record's are therefore both run with `--keep-going`: no red step stops either, the suite is one step, and what each names red is all that is red. **The record script refuses a red gate that was not run that way** — as the candidate's gate and as a record's alike — so the comparison is never made on half a list; a green gate ran every test however it was run.

**Its bounds.** A candidate whose *build* is red — the binary's, or the tests', which such a gate builds as a step of its own — runs no test on either tree: what the records would turn red there is seen first on a candidate that builds. The commit step reads the gate's output from the file the step tool kept it in, and trusts that the file is that run's: nothing ties a gate's output to a tree. And a flaky test shows as newly red — the halt the ruling intends.

## A round's `fix`

```text
Workflow({ scriptPath: '<the repository's root>/.claude/workflows/stabilize.js', args: { stage: 'fix', run: '<run>', scratch: '<dir>', rulings: [ … ] } })
```

**This stage refuses to start** (→ What is built, and what is not): what follows is what the rulings require of it and what its code was read to do before its repair, and nothing of it is used. `rulings` only when the human ruled. The stage starts from the loop branch or from the round's own branch, whichever is checked out.

**What the stage does**:

1. **The git state and the run's state**; the round's branch found, if it exists.
2. **The human's rulings recorded**, by one step.
3. **A cycle**, while a finding is routed to a fixer and the bound allows:
   - the **round branch** is opened from the loop branch, or switched to;
   - **fixers**, one per area — the blockers partitioned by the registry their door comes from — **serial, in the main tree, one finding per commit**, each commit behind the full gate. The lane is the completion workflow's; a fixer's return also carries the doors its commits can change, which the next round's scope step starts from. A fix that needs a new stored file, registry, flag or finding code, or that would revise a settled decision, is that fixer's halt for that finding and never its build decision;
   - the round branch is pushed and **the round tip's binary** built;
   - **the audit of the fix diff**, as exactly that diff over the product paths: a review and a drive, in parallel;
   - triage and verify-real of what the audit found and of what the fixers left open; the record step on the **round branch**; the push.
4. **Land** — only when the audit of the round's fix diff is green and nothing of the round is left open (ruling 7): the round branch is merged into the loop branch with a merge commit, and the loop branch is pushed. **What lands must be what was audited.** The step checks that the merged tree's product paths are exactly the round's; that the last audit read the round's tip as it then stands is the half of the requirement the review found unheld (→ Open questions).

**A cycle in which a fixer halted on a fork, or in which nothing was fixed, is not counted against the bound**, so that the same fixer is not sent at the same finding until a bound is reached. A fork stops the stage for the human; a cycle that fixed nothing returns `halted` with each finding the fixer could not fix and its notes. What ruling 7 requires beside that: a fix another fixer committed in such a cycle is part of the round's fix diff all the same, and is audited before the round lands — the first item of the review's list.

**What comes back from a landing**: `status: 'landed'`, the new candidate — the merge commit — the cycles it took, and `doors_for_retest`. `next` is then `test`, or `stop` where the run stops after every round.

**The `fix` stage's records ride the round branch** and reach the loop branch when the round lands (adjustment iv). So the state is the checked-out branch's: on the loop branch, a round that is out still shows its blockers open.

## Bounds and exits

**Inside a round: at most three fix → audit cycles** (ruling 6). **The bound is a fact of the run, and the state answers at it**: where a round's record counts as many cycles as the bound allows and a blocker is left, `next` is `stop` with `stop.why: 'cycle-bound'`, and the `fix` stage is refused (`cycle-bound`). The human raises it with `rulings: [{ cycles: N }]` — recorded, taken only at that stop, and standing for the rest of the run — or leaves the round, or rules what is open. **The `fix` stage's own handling of the bound is older and unchanged**: it counts against its constant, returns `halted` with `phase: 'bound'`, **the round unlanded**, and spells the three exits below as invocations, of which *continue* carries the raise as an argument nothing remembers. That stage refuses to start; which of the two it keeps is its repair's. Only the human takes an exit:

| Exit | The next `fix` invocation carries | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **continue** | `raise: { cycles: N }` | the bound for this round is N. Nothing remembers it: it is passed again on every later `fix` of that round. |
| **drop the round** | `exit: 'drop'` | the round's record says so; every row its fixes had closed is open again, because those commits never land; and exactly the round's record commits are carried over to the loop branch, so the round keeps its record. |
| **land a part** | `exit: { part: [ <the fix commits to keep> ] }` | the fix commits the human keeps, and the round's record commits, are re-cut as a branch of their own from the loop branch's tip, then gated and **audited once more as exactly that diff before it lands**. The fixes left out never land, so their findings are open again, and a part that lands ends the round. This exit is on the review's list (→ Open questions). |

**Across rounds: the stop mode, written at the opening.**

- **A run that stops after every round** returns `next: 'stop'` with `stop.why: 'every-round'` once a round is over — whatever follows, `close` included. The human's go is a recorded fact: `rulings: [{ go: true }]` on either stage, loop branch checked out. That invocation records the go, starts nothing, and returns the `next` the stop was holding. A go costs a record step, so a full gate. **A go answers one stop**: it is recorded with the count of fix cycles the round had, and a round that is over *again* after one more fix cycle — a finding admitted after the go, and fixed — stops again and takes its own go. **And nothing but the go lifts the stop**: a write that would begin the next round is refused until then, and the stop mode is written once.
- **A run that stops at the bound** returns `stop.why: 'round-bound'` when one more fix round would start and as many rounds have a fix stage on record as the bound allows. **The bound counts fix rounds, never test stages**: the `test` after the last allowed fix round always runs, and so do a finished triage, the human's ruling on a list, and a clause's re-run. Only the human raises it: `rulings: [{ rounds: N }]`, which is the go — **taken at that stop and nowhere else**: a bound raised before anybody was stopped at it is refused.

A ruling about the run — a go, a re-run, one more triage, one more attempt, a raised bound — is its own invocation and is never mixed with a ruling on a finding. **The record script takes each only while its state asks for it** — and every other write only where the state takes it: the table is its header's (*WHERE THE STATE TAKES A WRITE*).

**At the first stop of the workflow's first run** the human also looks again at the performance levers that were left open, on that run's own numbers ([decisions-pending.md](decisions-pending.md) → *The performance levers left for the first stop*). Until the human rules otherwise, the full gate precedes every commit.

## Branches

The model, its names and its rules are [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) → Branches; this section says only what a run adds, and spells no name. **The harness mints every branch name of a run in one function, `branchName`, from the run's slug.**

- **The loop branch** carries the run. **A round is its own branch**, opened from the loop branch, pushed by name, and landed with a merge commit only when the audit of its fix diff left nothing open (ruling 7). **A part** re-cut at a bound is a third branch. No step deletes one.
- **The invariant is by path** (adjustment ii). **Product paths — the paths the published crates carry, and their tests — move only through an audited round's merge. Every other path** — the run's records, `.claude/`, `dev/`, `implementation/`, the logs — **commits directly on the loop branch behind the gate.** Which paths are product paths is one list in the harness. **The tests of the repository's own tooling — this workflow's fence and the record script's suite among them — are outside it**: they live in `tooling-tests/`, not beside the product's tests in `crates/cli/tests/`, so a change to one commits directly like the tool it tests, and the rule needs no list of exempt files (the human's ruling of 2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The stabilization workflow's build, reviewed red*, ruling 2; which suites those are, and the one still owed, is [dev-workflow.md](dev-workflow.md) → Gate, *Where the tooling's own tests live*). Every stage opens with a check of the loop branch's history since the opening record's commit: a commit that changed a listed path directly, or a merge that is not a round's by its subject, halts the stage. That check is a tripwire for direct commits; it shows nothing about whether a merged round was audited.
- **An agent may land a round branch** — the one merge this workflow adds to what agents may do ([release.md](release.md) → What agents may not do; ruling 8). Everything else there stands: no agent pushes to or merges into `main`, force-pushes, merges a pull request or pushes a tag.
- **The `stabilize/` branch type is not applied.** The branch rule refuses that prefix today, and the names the harness mints are of a type the rule accepts — checked with [`dev/branch-name`](../dev/branch-name) for a loop, a round and a part. The type of its own is owed as **one commit — the last on the first run's loop branch before its pull request**: the branch rule and its fence, the model's table, the release doc, the fenced *Never* paragraph in every agent definition, and `branchName` ([decisions-pending.md](decisions-pending.md) → *The `stabilize/` branch type*). When it lands, the two sentences of this doc that say *not applied* are what changes: nothing here spells a name.

## The run's record

One directory per run under `completions/artifacts/`, named for the run. The list of its files and who writes each is the record script's header; what the orchestrator needs to know of them:

- **The ledger — one row per finding**, for the life of the run (ruling 13). **The run's facts name every finding the ledger was handed** — its census, written by the call that adds a row — so a row that is gone is missed, and a row a hand added is no finding of the run. Every finding of every stage gets a row, whatever its grade; a finding found again keeps its key and inherits the human's ruling on it, but not a fix that did not hold — that finding is open again. Keys follow the grammar of the [seed ledger](../completions/artifacts/M55/seed-ledger.md), so that an open row can later be filed as one jigc doc ([findings-channel.md](../design/findings-channel.md)).
- **The clause table — one row per clause** of the closing condition: the items that judge it and where each one's run stands, the commit and the rounds of those runs, and green, red or void (ruling 16). **It is a view**: the record script renders it from the item results, in the same batch as the write that changes it, and no caller writes it. **A table that differs from what the results give — a status changed by hand, a row or the file deleted — is refused** the next time the state is read, by what differs. **Closing is computed from the results and the ledger, and remembered by nobody.**
- **Each round's results — one row per run of an item** the round's scope selected: the attempt, the commit it ran on, and green, void with why, or red with the key of the finding it is filed as. A re-run is one more row of the round that selected the item.
- **What the record script counts in a round's record**, beside the facts a stage writes there: how many runs of each item the results hold; which findings the round's triage has left without a grade or a verdict, and how many times in a row; and which attempt of a stage reached its record. No caller writes them, and each is what a deletion or a loop is held against.
- **The declared-bounds list and the test set** are tables of their own, which the opening record names.
- **Per round**: its scope — the doors on both sides — its triage record, **with the fork of a contested finding beside its verdict**, its facts, **the candidate's gate** — the commit, the verdict, and every step and test the full gate showed red on it — and every report of every agent, by stage, cycle and attempt — the marker each attempt of a `test` stage began with among them.

**Nobody is to write any of it by hand** — neither an agent nor the harness nor the orchestrator. The script decides the path, replaces host paths with the public placeholders, stops hard on the public-hygiene scan — a report is public on the next push — and refuses a line that would redden the one fence that reads every markdown file. **And what a hand wrote all the same does not reach a commit or the remote through a step**: the same checks run again at every report check, at the record's commit and at every push (→ The record step, *What is vetted, and where*). A table that is not the script's own, or a cell outside its vocabulary, is refused as corrupt the next time the state is read — **and so is a table that lacks what a record names**: a ledger row, an item's result, the reports of an attempt that reached its record. The refusal names what is gone.

## Close

**`close` is computed, never judged** (ruling 16). What the rulings require of it: a tested candidate — the latest round tested it, and nothing was fixed since; nothing open in the ledger; and every clause's instrument green on record, *with that evidence still good for this candidate* — a deterministic check's green counts only for the candidate it ran on, a hunt's green from before a landing only while every fix round since was followed by a round that resolved a scope of its own and none of those scopes reaches an item of the clause. `forbids_close` lists what the script sees in the way. **The review of the build constructed states in which the script answers `close` against those requirements** — over an instrument that never ran to its end, and over a fix no audit read. *The first is repaired (2026-10-06 — → A round's `test`, the derived clause); the second is not.* Until the repair is recorded, an answer of `close` closes nothing. **And one thing `forbids_close` cannot list, because nothing can hold it:** an in-scope item whose door no round's scope ever named — mistyped, or rightly never reached — owes nothing, and the run can close without it. The state names every such item (`never_selected`) and every included door no item of the test set names (`uncovered`); the human reads both beside the evidence before closing (→ A round's `test`, *The derivation's inputs are held* — a declared bound).

**What cannot close.** A clause that no item of the test set judges stays void and answers `unsettled` once nothing else is open. That is where a run stands whose condition names the port rehearsal while it does not exist, or the regression set while no item of the test set runs its first part (→ The regression set). The two ways out: the instrument is designed with the human, built, and takes its row in the test set — `item-set`, after which the state asks for its run, as an attempt of the latest tested round; or the closing condition changes, which is a new opening. **And a clause whose items no round's scope has selected** answers `unsettled` too: nothing it is owed is missing, and it has no green run on record. Its way out is a round whose scope reaches one of its items — the human may order one (`scope`) — or, again, a new opening.

**The order**, once `next` is `close`:

1. **The human closes, with the evidence in front of them.** The return's `evidence` gives, per clause, the round and the commit its instrument last ran on and how many **fix rounds** behind the candidate that is. The human may order one more `test` with `scope: 'everything'` instead of closing — the answer to the one dissent the rulings record (→ Why this shape).
2. **The closing acts.** On the loop branch, each commit behind the gate: the run's closing record and its fold-back, and — on the first run — the `stabilize/` branch type. And [`dev/clean-litter`](../dev/clean-litter), which commits nothing. What a run's fold-back holds is not ruled in general; for the first run it is listed by [the pass's record](../completions/artifacts/M55/fix-pass-rc25/README.md) → *What is owed next, in order*, step 3.
3. **The sync, the gate, the push, the pull request** — [dev-workflow.md](dev-workflow.md) → Before a pull request to `main`. The harness returns the first of these as `close.sync`: the orchestrator spawns it verbatim, on the model it names, and reads its return's `line` — the one line `dev/stabilize-step sync-main` printed: `merged` or `up-to-date`, or `halted` with the word it refused with; then the gate on the merged tree, the push by name, and the pull request. **The sync is the last thing the harness has a hand in**: its merge is not a round's, so — as the path rule's step is worded — a stage invoked after it halts there.
4. **The human merges the pull request.** release-plz puts the version on the release PR ([release.md](release.md) → The release PR).
5. **The checks on the release PR's head — before the human merges it** (adjustment iii). This is a procedure the orchestrator runs with subagents, and no stage of the harness:
   - **the packaged-tarball install** on that head — `dev/runner-faithful --commit <sha> tarball` ([release.md](release.md) → Verifying a publish);
   - **the packaged file set compared with the tested commit**, the expected differences being both crates' versions, the engine pin, the lock file and the changelogs — **no script does it today**;
   - **one upgrade arm from the previous release to the new version**, which no local candidate can exercise: what an upgrade triggers fires only when the version differs, and every local candidate carries the previous release's. The trial harness holds what such an arm is built from — two pinned images, and the check that they are two different trees ([completions/trial-harness/](../completions/trial-harness/README.md)) — and **nothing of this workflow composes them**.

   The release is of two crates, each versioned on its own ([release.md](release.md) → Versioning).
6. **The human sets the version where the derived one differs from the planned one, and merges the release PR.** Both are the human's alone; merging it is the go, and the publish follows ([release.md](release.md) → The release PR · Publishing).
7. **The registry proof** — `dev/runner-faithful registry '<requirement>'` with the install line's requirement ([release.md](release.md) → Verifying a publish · Installing). After the publish it is the only proof left.

## Halt and resume

**A stage that returned is never resumed.** Halted, stopped at a bound or finished, the next invocation is a fresh one and reads the state as it stands; it works the next attempt, whose number the state gives, so a re-run's reports never collide with a stale one — **once**: after two attempts in a row that left reports and no record, the next one is the human's to grant (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks, *A stage that is the human's*). `resumeFromRunId` is for a **killed** run only, and then with identical arguments — and its replay is unexamined: a fresh invocation after a kill is what is known to work (a declared bound).

**A halt names its phase and its reason**, and says whether it was transient — an agent that returned nothing after retries, which is infrastructure and no verdict about the run. **A halt says what the next invocation does**: a halt of a step carries the word the tool refused with, whose the state is and what leaves it (→ Reading the next step, *A step's refusal is its word*), and a halt before the stage changed anything says to send the same invocation again once the cause is dealt with. **For the `test` stage, invoking it again is right after every halt**, because every invocation reconciles first: it finishes a killed write, settles a batch whose commit was made, makes the push that is owed, commits a batch that is pending, or works the next attempt where none is (→ The record step, *A record act is repeatable*). **One thing about *the same arguments*:** a stage that was handed a `scope`, a `clause` or a `crossModel` and left its triage unfinished is answered `refused` when they are sent again — the invocation that finishes a triage takes none of them — which is why the canary's setup prints two argument objects (→ The small canary). What the record and the branches look like after a halt in the middle of a `fix` act — a landing, a carried record, a round branch — is still on the review's list; until that half's repair is recorded, such a halt goes to the human with the tree, the branches and the state as they stand. **Clearing a halt: the orchestrator decides, a subagent acts** (ruling 17). Where a dead agent left the tree dirty, the harness says what it may have left — uncommitted edits, commits on the round branch — and decides nothing about them; the build loop's classification of such a tree is the precedent ([increment-workflow.md](increment-workflow.md) → Halt and resume, the three tree states), and nothing rules that it binds here.

What a halt leaves, as the source has it — none of it observed in a run yet:

- **A reporter that left no report halts the stage only where the stage stands on it** — the first preflight, the scope step, triage (→ A round's `test`, *How an agent can end*), and for the first two at the report check, after the instruments ran; everywhere else what it was launched for is recorded as not done, and the stage reaches its record. Where a stage does halt, the reports already written stay untracked under the run's directory; the next record step commits them as an earlier attempt's, and nothing removes them.
- **An attempt is on record from its first step.** The marker that step writes is a report like the others, so an attempt that halted before any agent wrote is counted too. An invocation that is refused, that halts at its git state or its first read, or that stops after `state`, began no attempt and leaves nothing.
- **A `test` stage that stops after its scope step leaves the round's scope written and uncommitted** — after a halt, or after a `stopAfter` later than `state`. It is a pending write, as the stage's reports are, and the step that opens every stage admits it: **the next invocation runs the stage as the next attempt, the scope stands — a round's scope is written once — and the record commit holds both attempts' reports.** *[Until 2026-10-06 that step admitted reports only, and the next invocation halted on the scope file — the first item of what the builder of this doc found. The simulation drives the two invocations in a row.]*
- **Two different agents exhausting their retries** trips a breaker and the stage spawns nothing more: that is the shape of a rate limit, and the answer is to wait. The stage then halts at its next step — after its instruments, where they ran — with nothing recorded and the attempt counted: it does not record what it has (→ A round's `test`, *Not on the list*; a row).

## Orchestration

**The vehicle matches the work**: the opening and every ruling are human-led, in the main session; a stage is a workflow that runs without the human and returns. The main session only orchestrates (ruling 17): it starts a stage, reads `next`, brings the human what is the human's, and passes the answer on. Long reports are read by subagents that return a summary; a fork is put to the human one at a time, in plain text with lettered options.

**One role, one definition** — the harness's `ROLES` table says which: the preflight (`stabilize-preflight`), the scope step (`stabilize-scope`), triage (`finding-triage`), verify-real (`finding-verifier`), the reviews (`milestone-code-reviewer`), every drive (`milestone-e2e-tester`), the advocate (`robust-advocate`), the fixers (`build-fixer`), the record step (`build-executor`), and every git step and every read of the record (`build-git`, on Sonnet, like every git step of the build harness; each is one command of `dev/stabilize-step`, whose line the agent relays — [increment-workflow.md](increment-workflow.md) → Orchestration). Two roles have no definition and a prompt that is their whole contract: the independent drive of an advocate's proposal, and the cross-model pass. Each prompt ends with the paragraph every definition carries — what no agent may do with `main`, a tag or a pull request — held to the definitions' own bytes by the fence. A definition for the first is the human's to order.

**The contracts are inert outside a run.** Each contract a run adds binds on a labelled line of the prompt that the build harness does not send, so the same definitions serve a milestone build as they did; the fence holds each label to one spelling, in the script and in every definition that binds on it.

**Independence is what agents do not share**: a scratch root of their own under the invocation's, rigs of their own, no sight of another's report unless the prompt hands it over. What they do share is the one binary per candidate — built once, read-only, its hash asserted by every agent that drives it. It is a release binary, so the debug-only route fences are not in it; the gate is what runs them.

**Checking the harness without a run**: `args: { selfTest: true }` — invoked by the script's path, like everything else (→ Invoking the harness) — launches no agent and checks the script's own logic, its tables and not what a stage does; the fence runs the same wherever `node` is installed. What a stage does when its agents answer is the simulation's, below.

## The simulation of a stage

**The `test` stage is run by a test** (2026-10-06 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The `test` stage of the stabilization workflow, simulated*): `tooling-tests/stabilize_simulation.rs` runs the committed harness, unmodified, through a whole stage, on every gate. The review of the build had found that no committed test executed either stage; this is the simulation each builder had written and thrown away, kept — **so that a repair starts by making its finding fail in it.**

**What is real in it.** The harness script; `dev/stabilize-record` and `dev/stabilize-step`, through which every report, every row and every git act of the stage goes; and git — a throwaway repository with a bare remote, a fixed identity and fixed dates. A run is opened in it as → Opening a run says one is opened: by the record script's own calls and one commit.

**What stands in.** *The Workflow runtime*: a script (`tooling-tests/fixtures/stabilize-runtime.mjs`) hands the harness the globals a workflow script gets and runs it once per invocation. *Every agent*: a function chosen by the label of the call — a label nobody scripted fails the test. The git steps, the preflight, the scope step and the record step do in the repository what their definitions give those roles to do, by running the commands their prompt spells; every other agent returns what the test scripts and writes its report through the record script, under the name its prompt hands it. *What cannot run inside a test*: the long commands a stage holds — the full gate, the regression set, cargo — which are stand-ins that print what a scenario scripts, **started, held and judged by the real step tool**; gitleaks; the build of a trial image; and the instruments' own work. A "binary" is what the stand-in for cargo left and the tool placed, its hash the tool's and measured again by each stand-in that drives it. **And every command a stand-in runs is taken out of its prompt as it is spelled there**: a prompt that names an act the tool does not have, or a flag it does not take, fails the run.

**What it drives.** One whole stage over a test set with an item of every kind the harness has a chain for, a deterministic check and an item the round's doors do not reach: a finding inside the round's test set — contested, so a fork, which is then on record and ruled — one outside it, one the verifier refutes, a row seeded at the opening, and reporters that found nothing; from the git state to the pushed record, and then what the state says next. Beside it: a finding the verifier could not drive, the invocation that finishes that triage, and the one more that makes the finding the human's; **an agent of every kind in at least one of its endings — it dies, halts, writes and dies, or returns with no report; not every ending of every kind** — a step of an item's chain, the cross-model pass in each of its five ways to fail, the verifier, the advocate, the independent drive, triage, the scope step and both preflights; a hunt left void and its re-run; the act that begins an attempt, and its refusals; a record step whose gate is red with something the candidate's gate did not show, and the invocation that commits its batch; a red candidate, recorded; a batch taken back; a record step that applied nothing; a driver that drove another binary, and its item void; **a return that lacks a field the stage needs of its role** — one arm per cell of the harness's `NEEDS` that voids, the field left out and, for a hash, returned wrong, each beside its control, and the rest of a chain not launched; a stage stopped after its scope step, and the attempt that follows it; a red check, filed as a finding; a check that could not run, its re-run inside the round, and the invocations the state did not ask a re-run of; the human's go, and the human's rulings on a round's list; and what a stage refuses or halts on before it tests anything — an opening that is not done, a run never opened, a tree that is not clean, another branch, and the `fix` stage, which refuses to start and of which nothing else is asserted.

**What it establishes** — about what a stage *did*, never about how the script is written:

- **which steps and agents ran, in which order, each on which definition and model** — a golden (`tooling-tests/fixtures/stabilize-test-stage.trace`). **A diff of that file is a change to what a stage does**: it is adjudicated against the ruling that asked for it, and re-captured with `UPDATE_GOLDENS=1 cargo test -p jigc stabilize_simulation::` — which CI refuses — never re-pinned to make a run green;
- **exactly one report per reporter launched**, on disk and in the one record commit, which holds the run directory's paths and nothing else;
- **the ledger's rows and the round's triage record**, as the record script reads them back;
- **the pushed loop branch at the recorded head**;
- **the return the orchestrator reads**, and that its `next` is the record script's.

**The stand-in relays as the runtime was observed to** (the second repair plan's `K3`). An agent's structured return decodes the `\uXXXX` escapes of the line it relays, so the stand-in's git steps do that to every line of a stage's step: a line that holds one comes back with other bytes, as it did on 2026-10-07, and a digest — which holds none — comes back as sent. A scenario can have a step hand back the line the tool prints unasked, a digest with one character changed, or one with a character outside ASCII and its hash made to fit: each is caught as altered, and nothing of it is passed on. Every agent runs the reads its prompt names — an item's row, its doors, the untriaged rows, a pending batch — as the prompt spells them, and a run over a record written in dashes, quotes and arrows holds that none of that text is in any prompt or in the return. The arms of the steps' endings are driven beside it: a look that finishes nothing, the owed push made by the next invocation, a commit that failed, a commit step whose agent died, the same ruling sent twice, what would not have been written at the commit and at a push, git's lock, a commit that is no record's, a head that moved, a scope set aside, a re-run on the round's candidate — and, through the harness's own reader, what a held command's step says.

**What it does not establish.** *A stand-in for the runtime is not the runtime*: `parallel` runs one thunk after another, so nothing shows what two agents do to one tree at once; a return is held to its schema by a small validator of the simulation's own; an agent dies only where a test scripts it, and then on every try and never half-way through a command; nothing is resumed; and that the runtime accepts the script at all is not shown. *Scripted agents are not agents*: that a model runs the one command of a step and relays its line whole, writes a payload byte for byte, or reads its definition the way a stand-in was written to, is shown by nothing here. *And two roads of a stage that are no property of a stand-in and that no arm scripts:* no reporter leaves something open, so no second triage pass ever runs; and no step is asked for after the breaker has tripped. Each is a late halt of the stated list, and a row. **So nothing leaves the canary's list** (→ What is built, and what is not; Open questions → *To be shown by the canary, and by round 1*) — what the simulation adds is that the list is no longer the only place a stage's order of steps can be seen to hold.

**Where a stage meets a defect the review recorded, the simulation pins what the stage does today** and names the finding at the assertion; it repairs none, and the task that repairs one turns its assertion. **No pin of the `test` stage is left: the last, from the second repair's `X1` — a confirmed verdict without the previous binary's hash halted the stage — was turned by `X1b`, and is asserted as recorded with its regression fact unknown (→ A round's `test`, *A return that lacks a field*). Of the first review's, seven were turned, and are asserted as repaired** — the last two by the repair of what becomes of an agent that does not report: a fork is a row of the round's triage record and the human's until ruled, and the invocation that finishes an unfinished triage reaches its record (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks). The five before them: by the repair of the record step, a record step under a red gate, a record step that returns no evidence of its checks, and the scope of a stage that stopped (→ The record step · Halt and resume); by the derived clause, a red check that reached nobody — it is a finding now; and by the counters, the laps of a triage that is not finished — the halted laps are counted and after one more the stage is the human's, a finding left unverified a second time is on the human's list with the reason, and the grants and the ruling that answer both are recorded through the harness (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks). Every record of those scenarios is written through the harness; the simulation makes no call into the record script but the opening's and its reads.

**On a machine without `node`** the simulation cannot run. It then **fails under CI** and passes anywhere else — and says so where somebody reads it: [`dev/gate`](../dev/gate) names every test that passed without running what it tests, beside its verdict ([dev-workflow.md](dev-workflow.md) → Gate).

## The runtime probes

**Four facts about the runtime the harness runs in had been guessed at, and tasks were being cut on the guesses; a probe observes one of them** (2026-10-07 — [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The runtime probes of the stabilization harness*). Why they exist, what each one's agents do, and **which task or row each result changes** are the second repair plan's to say and are not restated here: [plan.md](../completions/artifacts/M55/stabilization-build/re-review-test-half/plan.md) → §5, *The runtime probes*, with what a failing probe moves in its §6.

**A probe touches no run, no branch, no remote and no file of the repository.** It is an invocation of the harness that names no stage: its steps are commands of [`dev/stabilize-probe`](../dev/stabilize-probe) and of no other tool, and that tool writes under `<scratch>/probe/` and nowhere else — it refuses a scratch root that is not there, is no directory, or lies inside the repository, judged on the real path. The `relay` probe's throwaway runs are repositories of their own under the scratch root, built from copies of the record script and the step tool. So the probes run from the main checkout, in the orchestrating session, with no clone.

**How one is run.** From the repository's root:

```
Workflow({ scriptPath: '<the repository's root>/.claude/workflows/stabilize.js', args: { selfTest: true } })                      — first, once: that the runtime takes the script at all
Workflow({ scriptPath: '<the repository's root>/.claude/workflows/stabilize.js', args: { probe: '<name>', scratch: '<dir>' } })   — <name>: required · relay · relay-document · payload · hold
```

`<dir>` is a directory that exists, outside the repository, and **fresh for every invocation** — `mktemp -d` under the session's scratch root: a probe's root under it is made once, and a second invocation over the same root is refused (`exists`). `hold` takes one more argument, `seconds` — absent, twelve minutes; `2100` holds for the regression set's thirty-five. Anything of a stage beside `probe` — a `stage`, a `run`, a `model` — is refused before any agent runs.

## The regression set

**The closing condition's second clause — *a working product others can rely on* — has a regression set for its instrument: no command that works on the previous release stops working.** The human ruled its shape on 2026-10-06, in two parts ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The regression set's first part, as a tool*, which carries the ruling as it was handed to the builder). **The first part is built; the second is not.**

**Part 1 — the previous release's own test suite, run against the candidate's binary: [`dev/regression-set`](../dev/regression-set).** One call:

```text
dev/regression-set run --previous <the previous release's commit> --candidate <the candidate's commit> --list <the list's path in the repository> --scratch <a directory outside the repository>
```

It reads the list out of the candidate's commit; builds the previous release's tests from `git archive` of its commit, into a target directory of its own; runs them on their own binary — the baseline; builds the candidate's binary from `git archive` of its commit, in the same profile; puts it at the path the old tests have compiled in; and runs the same test binaries again. **Every test that passed on the previous release's binary and fails on the candidate's must be a row of the list, and every test that fails on its own binary must be an exclusion row of it** — or the check is not green. The tool's header owns the steps, the list's form, the line it prints and its exit statuses; four things about it that an orchestrator has to know:

- **Both runs are made from the recorded build, never through cargo.** After the swap a plain `cargo nextest run` puts the previous release's binary back, without a word, and reads green — the trap the method was measured to have ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md), the same entry). So the build is recorded once and both runs are made from that record (cargo-nextest's `--binaries-metadata` and `--cargo-metadata`), **and which binary each run drove is proven by hashes** — taken at that path before and after each run, and carried in the line as `proof`. A hash that is not the expected one makes the run **void, never green**.
- **A green rests on facts of the two commits, and on nothing the tool was handed** ([DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *2026-10-07 — The regression tool's green rests on facts of the two commits*). `--list` is a path in the repository, and the list is read **out of the candidate's commit** — never the working tree's file, never a file no commit holds. A run records the list it read, by its path and its sha256, and a verdict asked over another list is void. Two commits that are one are refused. So a list cannot be checked before it is committed, and a row added is a new commit, a new candidate and a new run.
- **It answers green, red or void, as a deterministic check does** (→ Triage, the human's list and the forks, *A red deterministic check is a finding*). *Green:* every difference is on the list, and every test that failed on its own binary is on an exclusion row. *Red:* at least one difference is not on the list — named under `not_on_list`; each is a finding, graded and verified like any other, and never fixed by adding a row nobody ruled. *Void, with its reason:* a tool was missing, a build failed, the swap did not hold, the second run was not whole, the evidence is of another list — or **a test failed on its own binary and no row excludes it** (`excluded_by_no_row`): the run is still made to its end, so that one run names everything the rows are then written from. **A test drops out of the comparison only by a row; there is no tolerance.** A row that matches nothing is stale, named with why, and changes no verdict.
- **It is not a test framework** ([pinning.md](pinning.md) → §3, *No script-runner harness*): it defines no test and runs none. The tests are the previous release's, built by cargo and run by the gate's runner; the tool is the swap, the proof and the comparison of two of that runner's reports.

**The list of intended changes is a file of the candidate's commit, which the tool reads and no agent does.** One row per test, four fields with one tab between each: the test's binary, the test, what changed in one line, and a pointer — of **three kinds**. `commit:<full sha>`, a commit the candidate holds and the previous release does not; `ruling:<path>#<the opening words of one heading>`, read in the candidate's commit; and, for an **exclusion row** — a test that fails on its own binary, with why in its third field — `excluded:<path>`, a file of the candidate's commit that is the line of a run of this tool, against the same previous release, in which that test failed on its own binary. The two kinds of row do not stand in for each other. **A row that points at nothing is refused, and the list with it** — before anything is built. A row is added by whoever lands an intended change, with the commit or the ruling that made it intended; a difference with neither is what the check exists to find — **and who may add a row is held by nothing but review** (a declared bound). **Where the list lies is one path, which the harness names** (`REGRESSION_LIST`) and the candidate's commit must hold: `completions/artifacts/M55/stabilization-build/regression-set/intended-changes.tsv`, seeded for `fix/rc24-tier1` against `1.0.0-rc.24`.

**Three runs are on record, and the record says which is the current evidence for which candidate** ([regression-set/README.md](../completions/artifacts/M55/stabilization-build/regression-set/README.md), its first table). *Run 1*, green — for a candidate **before** the eleven fixes built ahead of the opening, over a list no commit held: evidence for nothing as the tree stands, and the call it was made with is refused by the tool as it now is. *Run 2*, red — the merged product on the old list: one difference off it, which belongs to a ruled fix. ***Run 3*, green — the current evidence, for the candidate `b36b8c93`, the product as merged at `b33bf39b`**: 22 differences, all 22 listed, and the 11 tests that fail on their own binary are exactly its 11 exclusion rows. A commit that changes a product path, or the list, is another candidate and wants a run of its own.

**What a green result establishes, and what it cannot see.** Green says that nothing the previous release's suite pinned *through the binary* moved, but for the listed rows. **It is a floor, never "nothing that worked stopped working"**:

- **Tests that run in their own process never reach the binary** — the unit tests of both crates, and every suite test that calls the library: about half of the previous release's suite. The swap cannot reach them.
- **It sees only the states the previous release's tests built.** A regression in a layout or a configuration no old test sets up is invisible — and all four regressions the audit of the fix pass found were of that kind. That is what part 2 is for.
- **A version bump breaks the old tests that read the package version**: they compare the binary's with their own, compiled in. Run the check before the bump, or list those tests.
- **An archive is no repository.** Eleven of the previous release's tests ask git about their own source tree, fail on their own binary, and are the list's exclusion rows — **and one of the eleven reaches the binary: coverage the check does not have.** Building the previous release from a clone would measure them again; that is designed and not built (a row).
- **Where an old test hands the binary the previous release's pack on disk**, the candidate runs with that pack and not its own.
- **One platform — the one it runs on — and one profile, dev.**

**How a stage runs it: as a held check — no brief, and no agent's reading.** An item of kind `held-regression`. The stage starts it itself, by `dev/stabilize-step hold-start --kind regression` over the previous release's commit, the round's candidate and the list's path, **after the candidate's gate and never beside it**; one agent asks after it; the step tool reads the verdict from the regression tool's one line, held to its exit status and to the two commits and the list that were asked; and the item's result is the verdict's file, which reaches the record with the two commits, the list's hash and what was excluded in it (→ Reading the next step, *A scripted check is a kind of item*). In a re-run it is held again, on the round's candidate. Its three recorded runs took between twenty-one and thirty-four minutes on a machine other agents used. **No run has an item for it yet**: that row is written at a run's opening (`item-set`).

**Part 2 is not built**: the success paths of every command, run in the ordinary configurations and layouts — line-ending conversion, hidden untracked files, a symlinked `CLAUDE.md`, linked worktrees, a worktree of a bare repository, a separate git directory, a submodule, an older git, a moved repository — on both binaries. Until it is, a green part 1 is not the clause's instrument whole.

## Why this shape

- **Candidates are local** (ruling 2; adjustments i and iii). A candidate is a commit on the loop branch, built from that commit: inside the loop there is no version, no tag and nothing that needs the human's merge. The rulings state that property and its costs, and no sentence of why beyond the entry's premise: a loop that runs without the main session, which comes in only for decisions. The costs, each answered: every local candidate prints the previous release's version, so evidence is keyed by commit and by the binary's hash; a loop that closes on one machine would meet a red required check at the pull request, so the candidate's CI result is read on every candidate; a trial arm runs on an image built from the candidate's commit and no longer on a published version, so the proof a registry install gave moves to the end; and what only a version change exercises moves to the release PR's head.
- **Scope shrinks** (ruling 10, and the human's answer to the dissent). Round 1 tests what the candidate changed against the previous release, and each later round only what the previous round's fixes reach — because **a repeated hunt over unchanged code finds new things by sampling**, and a loop that hunts everything every time does not end. The pass this workflow came from is the evidence: six blocking rows became fifty-seven commits, and each door studied hard yielded another finding ([ideas/gate-loop-workflow.md](../ideas/gate-loop-workflow.md) → *What made the rc.24 loop not converge*). **The dissent stands on record**: a cross-model reviewer held that scoping by door is not safe without one full run of every model-driven instrument before closing. The human ruled that out; what was worked in instead is that a scope defaults to *inside* where it cannot be bounded, and that the close shows how old each clause's evidence is — in fix rounds, where the dissent was promised commits (→ Open questions).
- **A hunt runs once per change, a check always** (ruling 10). A model-driven instrument samples, so its second pass over the same code is a new sample and not a confirmation. A deterministic check is cheap and is evidence about exactly the candidate it ran on, so it runs in full on every one — the gate, into which every confirmed and refuted repro is pinned, among them.
- **Findings outside the test set go to the human** (rulings 4 and 5). The test set is fixed before round 1 so that the loop has an end: in the pass before, each door studied hard yielded another finding, and nothing said which of them the run owed. And the list is the human's, whole, because triage is where a list shrinks unseen — an *out of scope* nobody ruled, a severity used as a filter — which is why *inside* is computed and a bound is always the human's.
- **Close is computed** (ruling 16; [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) → *The decision table's holes, closed*, decision 1). *Cannot close before each instrument ran* was stated and enforced by nothing, with no word for *could not run*. So closing is a function of the clause table and the ledger, a void or missing row forbids it — and it is reachable only from a tested candidate, since a landing leaves something no instrument has seen.
- **Two stages, each a fresh invocation** (ruling 9). A workflow takes no input while it runs, so every point where the human may rule lies between two invocations — a stop is a return, never an agent waiting. And each invocation reads committed state, because the loop this replaces lived in one orchestrator's head.
- **Bounded, with the exits named** (ruling 6). The ruling sets the bounds and gives no reason. The increment loop's bound of the same size does: thrash is a signal for a human, not for a fourth round ([increment-workflow.md](increment-workflow.md) → The loop, step 4).
- **It is the customer of the workflows beside it.** A fix is a dev-workflow task, a verdict is the completion workflow's verify-real, a pull request is the dev workflow's step. Cross-reference, never restate.

2. **A check that was void in its round is not run again inside that round.** In a re-run the working tree is not the round's candidate: a check the preflight runs from a brief is recorded void there, and so is a held gate, by a word (`off-tree`) — the regression set, which names its commits, is held again. Such a round ends at `rule`, never at `close`, and the next round, whose candidate is the tip, settles it. *Pinned:* the simulation's re-run on the round's candidate, and *a held gate that is due again is recorded void by a word*. *(Until the commit that finished `X1b` a held gate got no result there, and `next` stayed `retest`.)*
3. **Some verdicts are still an agent's reading**: CI's `ci-ok` for the candidate's commit, the packaged-tarball install, that the trial image is verified, the preflight's environment asserts, whether the cross-model tool answers — and any `check` item that is a brief. *Reach:* a word on record that the evidence may not bear; at a round's stop the orchestrator has a subagent read the evidence the preflight's report names — a check by hand. *Unpinned.* **Not on the safe side.**
4. **A push made by hand is outside the tool.** Every push a step makes is vetted first; one made with plain git is scanned by CI alone, and after it. *Unpinned, by its nature.*
5. **The step tool pushes a record's commit and nothing else.** A tuning commit on the loop branch is its author's to push by name, and no stage starts over one the remote lacks. *Pinned:* `dev_stabilize_step`, *a commit that is no record's is not pushed by the tool*.
6. **git's own lock is named and never removed**, and what a kill *inside* a git child leaves beyond it is the human's. *Pinned* for the lock; that git moves a ref whole or not at all is relied on.
7. **What the simulation cannot show.** Its runtime is a stand-in and its agents are functions: `parallel` is serial, an agent dies only where a test scripts it, nothing is resumed, and the relay is the decoding that was observed. That a real agent types the one plain call, runs the read its prompt names, and relays a digest above 10 KB or of an act other than `state`, is shown by nothing but a run.
8. **The prompt fence has three named exceptions, and holds nothing a role runs of its own craft**: the independent drive of a proposal, the cross-model pass and the re-cut of a part — and a reviewer's rig, a driver's commands on the binary, the preflight's read of CI, the tarball install, the trial image. *Reach:* a permission prompt can appear in any of them, and a stage then waits on it. *Pinned:* the fence's arm (s), red if an exception stops being real.
9. **Nothing tells a stale harness or a wrong repository.** A workflow invoked by its name runs the copy the session loaded first, and a stage runs wherever its session stands. Every invocation is by the script's path; a canary's run and loop branch exist in its clone only. *Unpinned.*
10. **The harness does not hold `args.scope`**: what bounds a round to the doors an invocation names is the scope role's rule. A door written in other words shows as an item never selected (bound 1).
11. **What still halts a stage after its instruments ran** — the eight rows under A round's `test` (nine until `X1b` turned `regression-fact`). *Reach:* a late halt, the instruments run again, an attempt counted, and after two the human's grant; two of them — any ending of triage, and one entry's text the record script refuses — can repeat on the next attempt. *Pinned:* the fence holds the list to the code and to this doc.
12. **At a clause that is the human's, the position still hands `test` the next round**; `next` does not ask for it. *Reach:* only an orchestrator that answers `rule` with `test` — a round no bound counts.
13. **The candidate's gate runs on a tree that holds the attempt's marker**, and may hold the round's scope and its report: what such a file turns red is on record as the candidate's.
14. **An attempt can be counted that tested nothing**: the step that begins an attempt, run twice, counts it; a report written for an attempt nobody began counts as one and is committed with the next record; which reporters an attempt launched is in no committed table; and a human's scope on a second attempt is not applied — the round's scope stands.
15. **Until the `fix` half's repair a direct caller is not held as a stage is**: while the opening is not done it can write a whole run, a writer can take a cell its reader then refuses, and the writers' table is a census of names.
16. **A row of the regression set's list is held to a pointer that exists, and to nothing that says who ruled it**; the check is a floor, and one of its eleven excluded tests reaches the binary.
17. **A record's gate is held to the candidate's by the names of what is red, never by how many tests ran.**
18. **Sizes.** One record carried 300 findings in the `payload` probe, in the form a batch had then; no number above is known, and an agent's own return is not measured at all.
19. **A held command is nobody's to end.** The tool kills nothing: a dead job's command may run on, two gates at once contend for one lock, a held check recorded void as still running may end green under its name with nobody reading it, and a build's work directory is never removed. *(A held check that is dead or still running is a counted run since the commit that finished `X1b`: void by a word.)*
20. **What is kept under a scratch root is found under that root only** — the commit step's kept answer, a held command's job. An invocation handed another root is answered `no-batch` where an answer was kept, and starts commands of its own.
21. **The replay of a killed run resumed by its run id is unexamined**; a fresh invocation after a kill is what is known to work.
22. **Nothing refuses `test` on the ground that it is not fit** — the harness refuses `fix` alone — **and `test` is sound for round 1 and what follows it without a fix**, no further (*Where the two halves stand*, above).
23. **Small edges, each on the safe side.** Agent-written text reaches other agents' prompts, and no shell. A verifier's `contested` left out of its return reads as *not contested*. The self-test checks the script's tables, not what a stage does. `dev/gate --quick` prints a gate's verdict line, which no agent reads any more. And [`dev/stabilize-probe`](../dev/stabilize-probe) has four LOW findings of its own, and no stage runs it.
**Declared bounds — true as built, and to be known by whoever runs one:**

- **A round's record does not say that its branch was merged.** On the round's own branch, once a cycle is recorded and nothing is open, the state reads as after the landing; only `position.fix.land` says that the landing is owed.
- **A re-run runs over the doors of the round that selected the item, and on that round's candidate** — built from its commit, never the branch's tip, which the round's record commits have moved. Its result row names that commit; the hash of the binary it drove is in its reports and in no table — a round's record holds the one of its test stage.
- **A deleted row is missed where a record names it** — a clause's row against the census, a ledger row against the run's census of the ledger, an item's result against the round's count of its runs, a recorded attempt's reports against the round's record. **Not held against anything**: a row of the declared-bounds list, an item of the test set, a report of an attempt that reached no record, and a round's scope once nothing of the round is tested. And two tables changed by hand *consistently* — a row and the count that names it — read as written.
- **While the opening is not done the record script takes writes from a direct call — never from a stage — and not a round's first files only**: any round's, a fix cycle and a bound (the re-review's `R9`). No stage starts before `next` leaves `not-ready`: the state's position refuses it, and the step that begins an attempt refuses it again, from the state, before anything is written. What is not held is the order of a hand's own calls: the record script takes a report or a scope for round 1 while the run's facts are not whole. Closing that in the script is a change to where its writers are taken, and to the fixtures of its suite, and is not made.
- **An invocation that halts before its attempt is begun began none**: at its git state, at its first read, or at the step that would have written the marker. Its halt went back to whoever invoked it, and the state after it is the state before it. From that step on every attempt is counted, whatever its agents did.
- **A reporter's death is known by its report, and a report is known by one check.** An agent the runtime gave up on that writes its report later leaves a file nobody launched: the record's own check refuses the batch, the stage halts there, and the attempt after it does not see that file.
- **A second preflight that fails voids everything it was asked for**, the checks it did run among them: one fault, one reason.
- **A result that came with no report is dropped, and so is what it found**: the item is void and is run again, which finds it again or does not.
- **A fix stage's record that writes a fix and counts no cycle moves no counter** — a cycle that was not audited. The `fix` stage refuses to start, and what a cycle leaves on record is its repair's.
- **The ledger, the declared bounds and the test set are held to no position of the run**: a finding, a disposition, a bound's row and an item's row are taken at any time, from any caller. **[2026-10-07: an item's row is still taken at any position — and is held to what the run holds: the census, and what a round has run of the item or owes it (→ A round's `test`, *The derivation's inputs are held*).]**
- **A row the ledger hands a fix cycle's triage is verified on a binary of the round branch**, not on the candidate the round tested.
- **A dropped round's record commits reach the loop branch by cherry-pick**, behind no gate of their own. **A part's kept fixes get new commits.**
- **The halts of the `fix` stage's own record steps still end with the sentence every other halt ends with** — *the same args* — though the step they halt in is the rebuilt one: that stage refuses to start, and its halts are its repair's.
- **A disposition is the human's by vocabulary only**: the script cannot know that a human ruled, and its header says so. Of a declared bound it refuses a row that does not say where the human ruled it, and no more.
- **What a round found again is as its triage record stands**: an entry recorded again in the same round replaces its row.
- **The bound on a round's cycles is in two places**: a fact of the run, at which the state stops, and the `fix` stage's own constant and argument, which that stage's repair reconciles. The bound across rounds and the stop mode are the record's alone. A candidate's label is in no record: it is derived from the round.
- **`admitted` is not inherited across a fix that did not hold**: a finding the human admitted, fixed, and found again is routed by its door once more, so one found outside the test set is on the human's list again. It is the safe side — nothing is fixed or closed unasked — and is recorded, not repaired ([decisions-pending.md](decisions-pending.md) → *What the repair's design sets aside*).
- **The record script refuses the one line a standing fence reads, by a list of one.** A second fence over every markdown file owes it a row; until then the stage's own gate is what catches it.
- **The gate's fast tier is cut by this machine's last measurement** ([dev-workflow.md](dev-workflow.md) → Gate). [`dev/gate --fast`](../dev/gate) is a pre-check and never a gate. A run's own two gates — the candidate's and a record's — keep going and have no tiers.
- **Three definitions a run launches still describe the gate as running a step that retired** — a stale sentence in the definitions, with no effect on what the gate runs.
