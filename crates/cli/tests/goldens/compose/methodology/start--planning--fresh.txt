Committing this plan lands the roadmap entry; it does not open the milestone
work-unit. The entry is the durable record of what you decomposed, the work-unit
is what sub-tasks are minted under and joined from, and no reference joins the
two — their ids are minted separately, from the same title. So once this task
commits, open the work-unit under that same title:

jigc milestone create "<the title you just used>"

Then seed its task list by hand, one call per unit of work your decomposition
names — `<milestone-id>` is the id `create` minted:

jigc milestone add-task <milestone-id> "<intent>"

By hand is the whole path from here, and the bound is worth knowing before you go
looking for a shortcut: `jigc milestone add-from-spec <milestone-id> <spec-addr>`
seeds one sub-task per `criterion` of a committed spec, while this milestone's
breakdown is prose in the roadmap entry's `decomposition` slot, which carries no
criteria — so there is nothing there for it to read, and the intents are retyped
from your own decomposition.
Land the change as exactly one logical commit. finalize commits the git index —
only what you have staged (`git add`), plus the docs it manages. Unstaged edits
and untracked files are left out of the commit; with nothing staged over a dirty
tree, finalize refuses. Anything still staged from BEFORE this task was minted
makes finalize refuse too (one blocking finding per carried path): unstage it, or
pass `--carry-staged` to declare the carryover deliberate.

To see what's left before committing, run `jigc task validate sweep-the-compose-surface` — it
previews part of what finalize gates on (this task's content findings, the
carryover gate, the owner-artifact causes that need no staging, and the
granted-but-unused changelog gate), without committing anything; the staged set,
promotion and the commit itself are decided at finalize.
